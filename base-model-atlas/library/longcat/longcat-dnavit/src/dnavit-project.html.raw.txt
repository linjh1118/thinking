<!-- ======================= OVERVIEW ======================= -->
<section id="overview">
  <div class="col reveal">
    <div class="eyebrow">Overview</div>
    <h2>Can a finite discrete vocabulary represent the richness of the visual world?</h2>
    <p class="lead">Language models are built on discrete tokens and next-token prediction. Multimodal models, by contrast, usually keep separate continuous encodings for perception and synthesis. dNaViT asks whether vision can follow the language path: one learned vocabulary of discrete codes that serves <em class="k">understanding, reconstruction, generation, and editing</em> alike.</p>
    <p>Building on a frozen pretrained vision encoder, dNaViT uses lightweight <b>residual modulation</b> to enrich semantic features with reconstruction-relevant detail. <b>Grouped product quantization</b> expands discrete capacity without lengthening the spatial sequence, while <b>dual-branch reconstruction</b> and a <b>progressive training recipe</b> balance semantic fidelity and pixel accuracy within the shared bottleneck. As a single representation, it performs comparably to or better than specialized models across 12 understanding benchmarks, 4 generation benchmarks, and an editing benchmark.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="col">
    <div class="ideas">
      <div class="idea reveal"><span class="n">01</span><h4>Residual Modulator</h4><p>Fuses multi-level features of a frozen encoder and predicts a zero-initialized scale-and-shift residual, adding detail without disturbing semantics.</p></div>
      <div class="idea reveal"><span class="n">02</span><h4>Grouped product quantization</h4><p>Splits each feature into G channel groups with their own codebooks: up to K<sup>G</sup> combinations at the same token count.</p></div>
      <div class="idea reveal"><span class="n">03</span><h4>Dual-branch, three-stage training</h4><p>Semantic and pixel decoders share one discrete bottleneck, introduced progressively to avoid gradient interference.</p></div>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<!-- ======================= METHOD ======================= -->
<section id="method">
  <div class="col reveal">
    <div class="eyebrow">Method</div>
    <h2>From a frozen encoder to a shared discrete vocabulary</h2>
    <p>dNaViT enriches the features of a frozen native-resolution encoder, quantizes them group by group, and decodes the same codes into both semantic features and pixels. Step through the pipeline below.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="wide reveal">
    <div class="afig" id="afig2" data-afig="fig2">
    <div class="afig-bar" aria-label="dNaViT pipeline"></div>
    <figure style="margin-top:0">
      <div class="frame afig-stage">
        <img src="assets/fig/dnavit.jpg" alt="Overview of dNaViT: representation encoder, residual modulator, group-wise vector quantizer, multi-group discrete tokens and vision transformer decoder" data-zoom>
      </div>
      <div class="step-text afig-text"><h4 class="swap"></h4><p class="swap"></p></div>
      <figcaption><b>Figure 2. Overview of dNaViT.</b> (a) A frozen representation encoder handles arbitrary resolutions, with ResMod injecting multi-level information before group-wise product quantization; the shared codes are decoded by individual ViT decoders into pixel and semantic reconstructions. (b) ResMod fuses intermediate features and predicts a zero-initialized scale-and-shift residual. (c) Pixel and semantic branches are optimized with image and feature reconstruction losses.</figcaption>
    </figure>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="col reveal">
    <h3>Residual Modulator</h3>
    <p>Quantization can collapse weakly expressed appearance differences into the same code. ResMod exposes complementary cues from several encoder depths while keeping the final semantic representation <span class="tex">\mathbf{S}</span> as an anchor. Its affine head is zero-initialized, so at the start of training <span class="tex">\mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{S}</span> exactly.</p>
    <div class="eq"><div class="texd">(\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\beta}) = \operatorname{Split}\big(\mathcal{W}_{\theta}(\operatorname{LN}(\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(\mathbf{U})))\big),\qquad \mathbf{Z} = \mathbf{S} + \underbrace{\boldsymbol{\gamma}\odot\mathbf{S} + \boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\theta}}</div></div>

    <h3>Grouped product quantization</h3>
    <p>Each position's feature <span class="tex">\mathbf{z}_t\in\mathbb{R}^C</span> is split into <span class="tex">G</span> groups, each matched to the nearest entry of its own <span class="tex">K</span>-entry codebook. Capacity grows with <span class="tex">G</span> while the number of spatial tokens <span class="tex">N_q</span> stays fixed.</p>
    <div class="eq"><div class="texd">|\mathcal{K}| = K^{G},\qquad B_{\text{position}} = G\log_2 K,\qquad B_{\text{image}} = N_q\, G\log_2 K</div></div>
  </div>

  <div class="wide reveal">
    <div class="afig" id="afig3" data-afig="fig3">
    <div class="afig-bar"></div>
    <figure style="margin-top:0">
      <div class="frame afig-stage">
        <img src="assets/fig/group_tokenizer.jpg" alt="Tokenization and unified codes for understanding, reconstruction, generation and editing" data-zoom>
      </div>
      <div class="step-text afig-text"><h4 class="swap"></h4><p class="swap"></p></div>
      <figcaption><b>Figure 3. Tokenization and unified codes for downstream tasks.</b> (a) At each spatial position, the modulated feature is split into <i>G</i> channel groups and quantized with group-specific codebooks, yielding one index per group and their embeddings. (b) Understanding supplies only code indices to the language model (LM) through newly initialized group-wise embedding tables, trained jointly with the LM. (c) Reconstruction concatenates the indexed embeddings from the frozen tokenizer codebooks and feeds them to the pixel decoder. (d) Generation and editing use a multi-sub-token head atop the LM to predict each position's multi-level indices, optionally conditioned on a reference image, then decode through the same frozen codebooks and pixel decoder.</figcaption>
    </figure>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="col reveal">
    <h3>Dual-branch reconstruction</h3>
    <p>The same quantized embeddings <span class="tex">\mathbf{C}</span> feed two decoders. The <b>semantic branch</b> regresses the post-ResMod features with a cosine plus MSE loss; the MSE term guards against anisotropic features where cosine alone looks deceptively small. The <b>pixel branch</b> unmerges tokens back to the native grid and is trained with L1, MSE, LPIPS, ConvNeXt perceptual, and Gram losses.</p>
    <div class="eq"><div class="texd">\mathcal{L}_{\text{dual}} = \lambda_{\text{sem}}\Big[1-\cos\big(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}},\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf{Z}^{\star})\big) + \lambda^{\text{sem}}_{\text{MSE}}\big\|\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}-\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf{Z}^{\star})\big\|_2^2\Big] + \lambda_{\text{pix}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{pix}}</div></div>
  </div>
  <div class="wide reveal">
    <figure>
      <div class="frame"><img src="assets/fig/Dual_Branch.jpg" alt="Semantic feature cosine similarity map and pixel reconstructions from dNaViT-G16 and G64" loading="lazy" data-zoom></div>
      <figcaption><b>Figure 4. Semantics and pixel detail in one discrete representation.</b> (a) Reconstructed semantic features closely match the original QwenViT features (per-token cosine similarity). (b) Pixel reconstructions from dNaViT-G16 and dNaViT-G64, with crops highlighting fine text on the jersey.</figcaption>
    </figure>
  </div>

  <div class="col reveal">
    <h3>Refiner: a code-conditioned flow decoder</h3>
    <p>Discrete codes only partially specify fine appearance, so a direct ViT pixel decoder tends to over-smooth. The Refiner replaces it with a flow decoder built on a pretrained image-generation prior, conditioned on the codes through global context tokens and time-conditioned spatial residuals. A multi-step teacher trained by flow matching is distilled into a one-step student with adversarial supervision. The codes themselves do not change.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="wide reveal">
    <div class="afig" id="afig5" data-afig="fig5">
    <div class="afig-bar"></div>
    <figure style="margin-top:0">
      <div class="frame afig-stage">
        <img src="assets/fig/refiner.jpg" alt="Overview of the code-conditioned Refiner with teacher and one-step student" data-zoom>
      </div>

<!-- ======================= TRAINING ======================= -->
<section id="training">
  <div class="col reveal">
    <div class="eyebrow">Training</div>
    <h2>Three stages: continuous first, discrete second, joint last</h2>
    <p>The tokenizer pairs a pretrained Qwen3-VL ViT (~400M parameters) with a residual modulator (~100M), about 500M parameters in total. The ViT stays frozen throughout. Learning complementary detail is separated from adapting it to the discrete bottleneck, so pixel gradients never disrupt the semantic codes while they are still forming.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="wide reveal">
    <figure>
      <div class="frame"><img src="assets/fig/training_stage.jpg" alt="Three-stage training schedule of dNaViT" loading="lazy" data-zoom></div>
      <figcaption><b>Figure 6. Three-stage training schedule of dNaViT.</b> Continuous pixel pretraining is followed by discrete semantic reconstruction and joint reconstruction. Stage 2 stops pixel gradients at the quantized embeddings. Stage 3 opens this gradient path while a frozen copy of the Stage-2 ResMod supplies the feature target. Flame and snowflake icons mark trainable and frozen modules; dashed purple paths denote fixed targets.</figcaption>
    </figure>
    <div class="stages">
      <div class="stg"><small>Stage 1</small><h4>Continuous pixel pretraining</h4><div class="badges"><span class="badge hot">Trainable · ResMod, pixel decoder</span><span class="badge cold">Frozen · ViT</span></div><p>ResMod's output <i>Z</i> goes straight to the pixel decoder, bypassing the quantizer. Minimizing the pixel loss trains ResMod and the pixel decoder together, so the residual pathway learns to expose reconstruction-relevant information before a finite vocabulary is introduced.</p></div>
      <div class="stg"><small>Stage 2</small><h4>Discrete semantic reconstruction</h4><div class="badges"><span class="badge hot">Trainable · quantizer, both decoders</span><span class="badge cold">Frozen · ViT, ResMod</span></div><p>ViT and ResMod are frozen; their output <i>Z</i> is both the quantizer input and the fixed semantic target <i>Z</i>*. The quantizer and semantic decoder learn to reconstruct it, while the pixel decoder trains on detached codes, so pixel gradients cannot disrupt the codes while they are initialized.</p></div>
      <div class="stg"><small>Stage 3</small><h4>Joint reconstruction</h4><div class="badges"><span class="badge hot">Trainable · student ResMod, quantizer, decoders</span><span class="badge cold">Frozen · ViT, Stage-2 ResMod copy</span></div><p>ResMod is unfrozen as a student while a frozen Stage-2 copy keeps supplying the target <i>Z</i>*, preventing target drift. Removing the stop-gradient lets pixel gradients reach the quantizer and the student ResMod under the joint dual-branch loss.</p></div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="col reveal">
    <h3>Data</h3>
    <p>The tokenizer is trained on images only, combining general-domain collections (LAION, COYO, DataComp, Recap-DataComp-1B, CapsFusion and in-house data), text-rich images and documents (TextAtlas5M subsets, Chinese and English book and news pages, infographics, ChartGalaxy), and fine-grained or high-aesthetic images (face-focused LAION subsets, SA-1B-derived images). Training starts at a fixed 256×256 and moves to native resolution up to 2K with a balanced resolution mix, over several hundred billion tokens in total.</p>
  </div>
</section>
