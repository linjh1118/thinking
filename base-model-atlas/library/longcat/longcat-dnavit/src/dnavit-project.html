d NaViT 
Discrete Codes are General-Purpose Visual Representations 
Meituan LongCat Team A Discrete Native-resolution Vision Transformer Paper Code Models BibTeX Figure 1. dNaViT: a unified visual tokenizer for general-purpose modeling. dNaViT represents arbitrary-resolution images as discrete visual primitives, natively unifying reconstruction, understanding, generation, and editing under a shared vocabulary. By achieving performance competitive with specialized baselines across all domains, dNaViT validates a shared token-based paradigm for vision, analogous to language modeling. Can a finite discrete vocabulary represent the richness of the visual world? 
Language models are built on discrete tokens and next-token prediction. Multimodal models, by contrast, usually keep separate continuous encodings for perception and synthesis. dNaViT asks whether vision can follow the language path: one learned vocabulary of discrete codes that serves understanding, reconstruction, generation, and editing alike. 
Building on a frozen pretrained vision encoder, dNaViT uses lightweight residual modulation to enrich semantic features with reconstruction-relevant detail. Grouped product quantization expands discrete capacity without lengthening the spatial sequence, while dual-branch reconstruction and a progressive training recipe balance semantic fidelity and pixel accuracy within the shared bottleneck. As a single representation, it performs comparably to or better than specialized models across 12 understanding benchmarks, 4 generation benchmarks, and an editing benchmark. 01 Residual Modulator 
Fuses multi-level features of a frozen encoder and predicts a zero-initialized scale-and-shift residual, adding detail without disturbing semantics. 02 Grouped product quantization 
Splits each feature into G channel groups with their own codebooks: up to K G combinations at the same token count. 03 Dual-branch, three-stage training 
Semantic and pixel decoders share one discrete bottleneck, introduced progressively to avoid gradient interference. Method 
From a frozen encoder to a shared discrete vocabulary 
dNaViT enriches the features of a frozen native-resolution encoder, quantizes them group by group, and decodes the same codes into both semantic features and pixels. Step through the pipeline below. 
Figure 2. Overview of dNaViT. (a) A frozen representation encoder handles arbitrary resolutions, with ResMod injecting multi-level information before group-wise product quantization; the shared codes are decoded by individual ViT decoders into pixel and semantic reconstructions. (b) ResMod fuses intermediate features and predicts a zero-initialized scale-and-shift residual. (c) Pixel and semantic branches are optimized with image and feature reconstruction losses. 
Residual Modulator 
Quantization can collapse weakly expressed appearance differences into the same code. ResMod exposes complementary cues from several encoder depths while keeping the final semantic representation \mathbf{S} as an anchor. Its affine head is zero-initialized, so at the start of training \mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{S} exactly. (\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\beta}) = \operatorname{Split}\big(\mathcal{W}_{\theta}(\operatorname{LN}(\mathcal{P}_{\theta}(\mathbf{U})))\big),\qquad \mathbf{Z} = \mathbf{S} + \underbrace{\boldsymbol{\gamma}\odot\mathbf{S} + \boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\theta}} 
Grouped product quantization 
Each position's feature \mathbf{z}_t\in\mathbb{R}^C is split into G groups, each matched to the nearest entry of its own K -entry codebook. Capacity grows with G while the number of spatial tokens N_q stays fixed. |\mathcal{K}| = K^{G},\qquad B_{\text{position}} = G\log_2 K,\qquad B_{\text{image}} = N_q\, G\log_2 K 
Figure 3. Tokenization and unified codes for downstream tasks. (a) At each spatial position, the modulated feature is split into G channel groups and quantized with group-specific codebooks, yielding one index per group and their embeddings. (b) Understanding supplies only code indices to the language model (LM) through newly initialized group-wise embedding tables, trained jointly with the LM. (c) Reconstruction concatenates the indexed embeddings from the frozen tokenizer codebooks and feeds them to the pixel decoder. (d) Generation and editing use a multi-sub-token head atop the LM to predict each position's multi-level indices, optionally conditioned on a reference image, then decode through the same frozen codebooks and pixel decoder. 
Dual-branch reconstruction 
The same quantized embeddings \mathbf{C} feed two decoders. The semantic branch regresses the post-ResMod features with a cosine plus MSE loss; the MSE term guards against anisotropic features where cosine alone looks deceptively small. The pixel branch unmerges tokens back to the native grid and is trained with L1, MSE, LPIPS, ConvNeXt perceptual, and Gram losses. \mathcal{L}_{\text{dual}} = \lambda_{\text{sem}}\Big[1-\cos\big(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}},\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf{Z}^{\star})\big) + \lambda^{\text{sem}}_{\text{MSE}}\big\|\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}-\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf{Z}^{\star})\big\|_2^2\Big] + \lambda_{\text{pix}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{pix}} Figure 4. Semantics and pixel detail in one discrete representation. (a) Reconstructed semantic features closely match the original QwenViT features (per-token cosine similarity). (b) Pixel reconstructions from dNaViT-G16 and dNaViT-G64, with crops highlighting fine text on the jersey. 
Refiner: a code-conditioned flow decoder 
Discrete codes only partially specify fine appearance, so a direct ViT pixel decoder tends to over-smooth. The Refiner replaces it with a flow decoder built on a pretrained image-generation prior, conditioned on the codes through global context tokens and time-conditioned spatial residuals. A multi-step teacher trained by flow matching is distilled into a one-step student with adversarial supervision. The codes themselves do not change. 
Figure 5. The code-conditioned Refiner. Code embeddings condition a flow decoder through global context tokens and time-conditioned spatial residuals; a multi-step teacher is distilled into an efficient one-step student with adversarial supervision. Increasing the group count (G64) improves reconstruction fidelity, while fewer groups (G16) with the one-step Refiner preserve fidelity and improve perceptual quality. Training 
Three stages: continuous first, discrete second, joint last 
The tokenizer pairs a pretrained Qwen3-VL ViT (~400M parameters) with a residual modulator (~100M), about 500M parameters in total. The ViT stays frozen throughout. Learning complementary detail is separated from adapting it to the discrete bottleneck, so pixel gradients never disrupt the semantic codes while they are still forming. Figure 6. Three-stage training schedule of dNaViT. Continuous pixel pretraining is followed by discrete semantic reconstruction and joint reconstruction. Stage 2 stops pixel gradients at the quantized embeddings. Stage 3 opens this gradient path while a frozen copy of the Stage-2 ResMod supplies the feature target. Flame and snowflake icons mark trainable and frozen modules; dashed purple paths denote fixed targets. Stage 1 Continuous pixel pretraining Trainable · ResMod, pixel decoder Frozen · ViT 
ResMod's output Z goes straight to the pixel decoder, bypassing the quantizer. Minimizing the pixel loss trains ResMod and the pixel decoder together, so the residual pathway learns to expose reconstruction-relevant information before a finite vocabulary is introduced. Stage 2 Discrete semantic reconstruction Trainable · quantizer, both decoders Frozen · ViT, ResMod 
ViT and ResMod are frozen; their output Z is both the quantizer input and the fixed semantic target Z *. The quantizer and semantic decoder learn to reconstruct it, while the pixel decoder trains on detached codes, so pixel gradients cannot disrupt the codes while they are initialized. Stage 3 Joint reconstruction Trainable · student ResMod, quantizer, decoders Frozen · ViT, Stage-2 ResMod copy 
ResMod is unfrozen as a student while a frozen Stage-2 copy keeps supplying the target Z *, preventing target drift. Removing the stop-gradient lets pixel gradients reach the quantizer and the student ResMod under the joint dual-branch loss. 
Data 
The tokenizer is trained on images only, combining general-domain collections (LAION, COYO, DataComp, Recap-DataComp-1B, CapsFusion and in-house data), text-rich images and documents (TextAtlas5M subsets, Chinese and English book and news pages, infographics, ChartGalaxy), and fine-grained or high-aesthetic images (face-focused LAION subsets, SA-1B-derived images). Training starts at a fixed 256×256 and moves to native resolution up to 2K with a balanced resolution mix, over several hundred billion tokens in total. Results

Reconstruction 
Same codes, sharper pixels 
With visual representation and input resolution fixed, the ViT pixel decoder reliably preserves layout, color, and semantics but over-smooths high-frequency detail. The Refiner, conditioned on the same codes, recovers sharper letter contours and textures, which shows the codes carry more information than direct pixel decoding recovers.
