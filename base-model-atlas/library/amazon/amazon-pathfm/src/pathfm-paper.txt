Abstract
Time series foundation models are becoming increasingly popular for general-purpose forecasting.
However, most leading models predict marginal quantiles, leaving the dependence structure of future trajectories unspecified.
This distinction matters for trajectory-dependent quantities such as cumulative demand, peak load, and event timing, yet point and marginal scores do not reveal it.
To model this dependence, we introduce PathFM, a trajectory-based forecasting model that uses flow matching to generate complete future paths.
We further distill the model to one or two steps and share history attention cache across draws.
Together, these enable PathFM to generate multiple paths for a single series in roughly the time needed for one query to a quantile model.
The distilled sampler also makes it practical to optimize a calibration loss directly.
PathFM achieves competitive marginal accuracy on the established forecasting benchmarks fev-bench and TIME.
To complement these evaluations, we introduce Forecast Path Score Bench (fps-bench), which augments fev-bench tasks with energy and variogram scores and downstream metrics.
PathFM achieves a higher aggregate path score on fps-bench than all evaluated external baselines.
Experiments on chaotic systems further show that our flow model sustains long-run variability, while strong baselines rapidly collapse.
PathFM makes generative time series forecasting both competitive and practical, enabling diverse downstream applications.

Introduction
Time series foundation models (TSFMs) are a hot topic
(
Ansari et al., 2024
;
Das et al., 2023
;
Ansari et al., 2025
;
Jain & Sen, 2026
;
Cohen et al., 2025
)
.
The field is growing fast:
Every year, new models are released that refresh the leaderboard.
The models are commonly evaluated through point accuracy (e.g., MASE) and marginal probabilistic scores (e.g., CRPS, scaled quantile loss)
(
Aksu et al., 2024
;
Shchur et al., 2025
)
.
Consequently, most of the recent TSFMs are designed and trained to produce quantile levels, which are optimized for these benchmarks.
Point accuracy and marginal probabilistic scores are widely adopted for good reason: they provide an interpretable, per-step summary of forecast quality that directly supports common operational decisions such as inventory planning, resource allocation, and risk management.
However, they do not capture all aspects of forecast quality.
Other properties, particularly
trajectory-dependent
ones, are important but overlooked by these metrics.
Among many, we illustrate two important aspects:
•
Learning distributions with multiple modes:
Quantiles are defined through the inverse of the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the target distribution.
These CDFs, however, are not always nice and smooth, for instance, when the target distribution has multiple modes or sharp peaks.
With a few or a few dozens of quantiles, the resulting approximation of the CDF can be quite rough and suffer from a regression-to-the-mean issue
(
Yu et al., 2026
)
.
See the Lorenz oscillator example in
Figure
1
.
•
Learning autocorrelation:
Many quantile regression models predict marginal quantiles at individual time steps across a forecast window.
These marginal outputs do not specify temporal dependence among future values within that window.
For instance, many forecasting problems also require learning trajectory-dependent quantities, such as cumulative demand, peak load, and threshold-crossing times
(
Perez-Diaz et al., 2025
;
Cai et al., 2026
)
.
See the solar-output example in
Figure
1
.
A marginal quantile loss does not identify these joint properties.

Left: a sampled Lorenz rollout sustains variability and a two-lobe structure, while the fine-tuned Chronos-2 trace contracts.

Ad hoc methods have been proposed to mitigate these weaknesses, such as completing marginal quantiles under independence or comonotone assumptions
(
Perez-Diaz et al., 2025
)
, or learning copula structures on top of marginal forecasts
(
Drouin et al., 2022
;
Ashok et al., 2024
)
.
On the other hand, diffusion- or flow-matching-based generative time-series models are naturally well-suited for capturing joint trajectory distributions even without these ad hoc methods, as they can generate complete future paths while maintaining temporal and cross-variable dependencies.
Unfortunately, existing generative models
(
Kollovieh et al., 2025
;
Liu et al., 2025
)
achieve neither high marginal accuracy nor efficient sampling, limiting their practical utility in real-world forecasting scenarios.
This leads us to ask:
Can we construct a generative time-series model that achieves both high marginal accuracy and efficient sampling while also preserving trajectory dependence?
We introduce PathFM to combine joint and multi-mode trajectory modeling with accurate marginal forecasting and efficient sampling.
The model learns temporal and cross-variable dependence through conditional flow matching
(
Lipman et al., 2022
)
, generating complete future paths conditioned on history and known future covariates.
Parallel decoding distillation
(
Shaul et al., 2026
)
and history attention caching enable faster sampling with one or two model evaluations.
To narrow the marginal accuracy gap, we add a calibration loss that directly optimizes fair CRPS on generated forecasts.
The distilled sampler makes this optimization practical by allowing calibration gradients to pass through its short rollout and refine the forecast distribution.
With these components, PathFM-300M ranks
second
on the widely used fev-bench for marginal scores (
Section
5
).

On Forecast Path Score Bench (fps-bench), which we curate to evaluate trajectory quality, PathFM ranks
first
for path scores (
Section
5
).
In a separate evaluation on chaotic systems, our sampling approach improves point forecast accuracy upon the state-of-the-art PANDA model, captures useful temporal dependence, and preserves long-run variability more faithfully than baselines (
Section
6
).
These results demonstrate that PathFM generates high-fidelity trajectory forecasts without sacrificing marginal accuracy, making it a strong candidate for forecasting applications.
Our contributions are as follows:
•
Fast and calibrated trajectory generation.
We introduce PathFM, a joint flow foundation model for fast, calibrated trajectory generation with accurate small-ensemble marginals (
Figure
2
).
Distillation enables one- or two-step generation, marginal-CRPS refinement improves empirical coverage, and stratified sampling reduces finite-ensemble marginal error.
•
A benchmark for trajectory distributions.
We introduce fps-bench, to our knowledge the first dedicated benchmark for evaluating path distributions from time series foundation models on real-world data.
It reveals how strong marginal accuracy in existing models can mask errors in temporal dependence and uncertainty over trajectory-dependent quantities.
•
State-of-the-art performance.
PathFM outperforms leading foundation models on downstream probabilistic forecasting tasks and stays among the top on marginal benchmarks.
It also achieves the highest forecasting accuracy across strong baselines on chaotic systems.

3.2
Efficient backbone for joint generation
The velocity field uses a transformer based on T5
(
Raffel et al., 2020
)
, with separate time and channel axes.
Its inputs are patches of 16 values with masks and time indices, a register token, and future patches populated by available covariates.
Each block combines temporal self-attention, attention across channels at aligned patches, and a pointwise feed-forward network.
We condition flow time via adaptive RMS normalization
(
Chen et al., 2024
)
and apply QK normalization and weight decay.
For reuse across paths,
late entry
separates conditioning from flow-dependent computation.
The first
e
e
blocks use unrestricted attention over history, the register, and future placeholders, without source noise or flow time.
These representations incorporate the requested horizon and known future covariates.
Restricting history attention before this stage would exclude that future information.
Future positions then receive a gated difference between flow-state and placeholder tokens.
In subsequent blocks,
history-only self-attention
keeps history independent of the sampled path: history tokens attend only to history, while future tokens attend to both history and future.
Flow-time modulation acts only on register and future tokens.
Section

specifies the configuration.

4
Evaluating Trajectory Forecasts
Trajectory forecasts should capture temporal dependence and accurately quantify uncertainty.
Marginal accuracy alone cannot establish this.
We introduce Forecast Path Score Bench (fps-bench) to evaluate these properties alongside marginal scores.
The benchmark combines established scoring rules into a common protocol for constructing and comparing finite trajectory ensembles.
Six complementary path scores.
Different downstream queries depend on different aspects of a path, motivating the complementary diagnostics in
Table
7
.
Following the aggregation-and-transformation framework of
Pic et al. (2025)
, we score both complete paths and transformations that expose these aspects.
Energy score assesses the distribution of level paths, while differenced energy score emphasizes local changes by scoring the initial level and subsequent increments.
Variogram score compares pairwise temporal contrasts to probe how values vary together across time
(
Scheuerer & Hamill, 2015
)
.
We then apply scalar CRPS to the horizon total, maximum, and first-passage time to assess accumulated uncertainty, extremes, and event timing.
Specifically, first-passage time records the first crossing of a history-derived threshold.
fps-bench evaluates 94 fev-bench tasks
(
Shchur et al., 2025
)
, excluding those with NaN values that prevent computing some path scores.
Quantile-model completions.
Applying these scores requires complete paths, which marginal quantile forecasts do not specify.
We retain native samples when available.
To include quantile models, we construct paths from their marginal quantiles under two explicit dependence assumptions.
Sorted quantiles define a piecewise-linear inverse CDF with constant extension beyond the outer quantiles.
An independent completion draws a separate quantile rank at each forecast time and channel, whereas a comonotone completion shares one rank across an item’s times and channels.
Both laws preserve the same underlying interpolated marginals, allowing us to examine how the dependence assumption affects trajectory scores.
These two choices serve as baselines, as different dependence assumptions may yield different scores for the same marginal forecasts.
Section
D.1
specifies the score definitions, estimators, and aggregation.

7
Discussion and conclusion
PathFM combines flow matching, distillation, shared computation, and direct score refinement to generate complete future trajectories efficiently.
Its strongest gains concern the quality of complete trajectories and the downstream predictions they support.
The dynamical study shows a unique benefit: independently continued paths preserve variability that point forecasts can lose.
Several limitations remain.
PathFM does not yet support conditioning on modalities beyond time series, such as text.
For chaotic systems, all four dynamical diagnostics remain well above the held-out-truth reference (
Table
2
).
Accurately recovering the invariant distribution remains an open challenge.
A key direction for future work is to continue scaling the backbone and training data, pushing the frontier of generative time series forecasting.
