RFD4-Proteina is available as a Python package on PyPI and as a Hugging Face Diffusers pipeline. Both need Python 3.12 and an NVIDIA GPU with a CUDA 13-compatible driver; the PyPI release and the Hugging Face repositories go public with the release. The quickstart in the docs covers the pip package, a git clone with make setup , and Diffusers.

import torch
from diffusers import RFD4ProteinaPipeline
from rfd4_proteina_prep import prepare_cif, write_cif

pipe = RFD4ProteinaPipeline.from_pretrained(
    "nvidia/RFD4-Proteina-Diffusers", dtype=torch.bfloat16).to("cuda:0")
features, atoms = prepare_cif("input.cif", seed=42, device=pipe.device)
output = pipe(features, generator=torch.Generator("cuda:0").manual_seed(42))
write_cif(output, features, atoms, "design.cif")

Go to validation details 1. The RFD4-Proteina Model Abstract 
Biomolecular design requires finding chemically and geometrically compatible arrangements of interacting atoms and molecules. The complexity of problems which can be tackled by deep learning design models is limited by both the molecular landscape encompassed by the model and the ability to explore that landscape effectively. Here we introduce RFD4-Proteina, which addresses these limitations through intrinsic and extrinsic reasoning in a shared, physics-informed chemical and conformational latent space. The latent space encompasses standard and modified amino acids, sugars, DNA, and RNA, supervised with force-field energies to favor physically plausible conformations. Within this space, intrinsic reasoning uses recurrent refinement to adaptively allocate computation, while extrinsic reasoning guides search with arbitrary constraints such as energetics or geometric criteria. Across in silico protein design benchmarks, RFD4-Proteina achieves higher design success rates at greater generation throughput than all methods evaluated, with further gains from conformational flexibility, recurrent refinement, and search. Without additional training, the same model performs equivalently to or better than state-of-the-art specialist methods for related tasks, including side-chain packing, fixed-backbone sequence design, conformational sampling, and design refinement. Experimental validation spans specific and cross-reactive binding to therapeutically relevant targets, modified and glycosylated proteins, and catalysis of both bond-breaking and bond-forming reactions, including a first proof-of-concept demonstration of a de novo designed enzyme for active pharmaceutical ingredient synthesis. By uniting diverse molecular chemistry, conformational flexibility, scalable reasoning, and physical modeling with machine-learning generation, RFD4-Proteina expands the frontier of protein design. Design methods usually trade accuracy for speed. Hallucination methods such as BindCraft reach good pass rates but produce only tens of designs per GPU-hour; diffusion models produce hundreds to thousands but pass less often. On all four benchmarks, RFD4-Proteina is better on both counts: even at its lowest thinking setting, it has a higher pass rate and higher throughput than every other method we compared. In the figure below, the green region marks methods that are better on both counts. 
What counts as a pass. We refold each designed sequence with AF3 and check it against the intended structure and task-specific criteria. The clustered pass rate counts distinct folds only, so repeating one success earns nothing. 
Better and faster. For protein binding, single-shot RFD4-Proteina passes 9.8% of the time versus 5.9% for the next-best model (BindCraft), at 58-fold higher throughput. For small molecules it is 3.7-fold higher than the next-best model (8.7% versus 2.4%) and passes for all 77 targets versus 64. For enzyme scaffolding it reaches 8.9% versus 5.7%, and for DNA binding 2.2% versus 0.8%. 
Thinking longer helps. From low to high thinking, the protein-binding pass rate rises from 7.0% to 9.8% while throughput halves, from 2,716 to 1,363 designs per GPU-hour. 
Search pushes further. Search guided by a structure predictor raises pass rates to 11.9% (protein binding), 12.9% (small molecules), 12.4% (enzymes) and 3.5% (DNA), at 19- to 57-fold fewer passing clusters per GPU-hour.

Calmodulin switches conformation when it binds Ca 2+ , and many of its partners recognize only one state. We asked RFD4-Proteina for binders to the calcium-bound form only. 
Hit rate. 12 of 18 designs bound with an apparent K D below 1 µM and showed no detectable binding without calcium. 
Affinity. Apparent K D from 4.24 pM to 37.0 nM. *The 4.24 pM value comes from a model-dependent fit to BLI data and is not a precisely resolved affinity.

A binder to a peptide–MHC complex has to read a few exposed peptide residues and ignore the conserved MHC around them. Cancerous and infected cells are flagged this way, which is why look-alike peptides from healthy cells are the main risk. 
Targets. MART-1, SARS-CoV-1 membrane-protein and Phox2B peptides. 
Affinity. Yeast-display titrations with pMHC tetramers gave apparent half-maximal binding concentrations of 340 pM, 710 pM and 1.60 nM. 
Specificity. Over 100-fold preference for the on-target peptide in all cases. The Phox2B and SARS-CoV-1 off-targets differ from the target peptide by only one and two residues. 
Figure 4b. Binders to MART-1, SARS-CoV-1 and Phox2B peptide–MHC complexes: design models, yeast-display titrations, and binding to the six peptide–MHC targets in the specificity screen. Full figure Disordered peptide binders 
Intrinsically disordered regions are involved in neurodegeneration, chronic pain and cancer, but they have no fixed structure to design against. We targeted eight disordered peptides: five known to fold when bound and three without that tendency. 
Hit rate. Tight binders for all but one peptide in each group. 
First binders. To our knowledge, the first de novo binders to the tested YAP1 and Tau epitopes. The strongest hits have SPR affinities of 112 pM and 527 pM. 
Specificity. All-by-all tests of the best hits against five targets showed a preference for the intended peptide in every case, with varying strength. 
Figure 4c,d. Binders to four disordered peptides (YAP1, Tau153, N17, PER2) with SPR sensorgrams and 1:1 fits (c), and all-by-all SPR specificity of the best binders against five peptides (d). Full figure TrkA: species and paralogue specificity C10 human only B8 human + mouse 
Trk receptors are targets in neuropathic pain and in cancers such as neuroblastoma. Human TrkA is an established benchmark, so we asked for more: binders that avoid mouse TrkA (86% sequence identity over the modeled region) and the human paralogues TrkB and TrkC. 
Human-specific. The four strongest hTrkA binders showed no binding to any off-target. 
Cross-reactive. 18 of 94 designs bound human and mouse TrkA but not TrkB or TrkC. The best was sub-nanomolar for hTrkA and 37.7 nM for mTrkA. 
Unconstrained hTrkA. 158 of 188 designs (84%) were sub-micromolar, against 18 of 60 for the next most successful campaign. 
TrkB and TrkC. One sub-micromolar TrkB binder (30.2 nM) and 20 TrkC screening hits. To our knowledge, the first designed binders to either. 
Figure 4e,f. Human-specific and human/mouse cross-reactive TrkA campaigns with BLI against human and mouse TrkA (e), and the experimental hit rate of unconstrained human TrkA design compared with other campaigns (f). Full figure Glycan shielding B10 
N-linked glycans are trimmed by enzymes in the ER and Golgi. Blocking that processing triggers an immune response to the underprocessed glycan, which makes it usable as a vaccine adjuvant. A design has to shield the core GlcNAc while keeping the terminal mannose accessible. 
Previous work. Designs retained Man5–Man8 glycans; none fully blocked processing to Man9 or Glc1Man9. 
In silico. Higher design success rates than the earlier RFDAA-based method. 
Plate-scale screen. Glycoproteins retained mostly Man9 and Glc1Man9. 8 of 9 designs had more than 90% oligomannose on occupied sites, with smaller proteins than before.

Phosphorylation regulates molecular interactions across all domains of life. We targeted three phosphorylated variants of the histone H3 tail: pS10, pT11 and pS28. 
Binders. Phosphorylation-dependent binders for all three sites. 
Selectivity. The pT11 and pS10 hits preferred their phosphorylated targets and stayed selective against phosphorylation at the adjacent site. 
Affinity. Half-maximal binding concentrations of about 138 nM (pT11) and 1.03 µM (pS10), measured on yeast without avidity. Natural readers of these marks report mid-micromolar affinities.

Paraoxon is an organophosphate pesticide and a potent neurotoxin. Our earlier RFD3 designs scaffolded six catalytic residues around two metal ions and reached k cat / K M of only 4 ± 3 M −1 s −1 . We added two catalytic residues, which makes the active site harder to scaffold. 
In silico. 111 of 1,000 RFD4-Proteina designs passed the computational filters, against 1 of 10,000 for RFD3. 
Screening. 89 of 288 designs were active, spanning 25 of 47 scaffold families. None of 192 RFD3 designs were. 
Best design. Of six characterized designs, the best reached k cat / K M = 3,600 ± 500 M −1 s −1 and k cat = 0.9 s −1 , a 1.5 × 10 7 -fold rate enhancement. All six beat the earlier phosphotriesterases by 10- to 1,000-fold. The numbers on the viewer buttons are each design’s k cat / K M in M −1 s −1 . 
Figure 6a–d. Paraoxon hydrolysis (a), the eight-residue active-site theozyme (b), in silico success rates for RFD3 and RFD4-Proteina (c), and four designs with their backbone and motif RMSDs and catalytic efficiencies (d). Full figure Ligase for lenacapavir synthesis Design 52 79% (S) 
Lenacapavir is an HIV-prevention drug whose manufacturing cost limits access. One step joins an activated ester to the ( S )-enantiomer of a hindered chiral amine, so the enzyme has to favor aminolysis over hydrolysis of the ester. 
Design. We scaffolded a cysteine-protease catalytic triad and used multi-state AF3 filtering to bias designs toward aminolysis. 
Hit rate. Four of 67 designs formed the amide above background. An earlier RFD3 campaign found none in 72. 
Best design. Design 52 converted 79.0% of the ( S )-amine and 8.8% of the ( R )-amine, with acid at the no-enzyme level (0.29 vs 0.26 mM). Design 58 gave 61.7% vs 14.6%. 
First. To our knowledge, the first proof of concept of a de novo enzyme for a step in active pharmaceutical ingredient synthesis.
