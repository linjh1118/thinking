Model Overview

Description:

The NVIDIA Qwen3.5-0.8B-Qwen3.8-27B-Draft model is an external draft model for Qwen’s Qwen3.8-27B – a complete language model rather than a draft head, sharing the target’s tokenizer and vocabulary. It proposes blocks of candidate tokens which Qwen3.8-27B verifies by rejection sampling, reducing decode latency without changing the target’s output distribution.

Trained with NVIDIA Model Optimizer in external speculative decoding mode.

This model is ready for commercial or non-commercial use.

Training and Evaluation Datasets:

Training Dataset:

Link: Nemotron-Post-Training-Dataset-v2. Only the prompts were taken from this dataset. Responses were synthesized by Qwen3.8-27B. Data Modality: Text Text Training Data Size: 1 Billion to 10 Trillion Tokens Data Collection Method by dataset: Hybrid: Automated, Synthetic

Labeling Method by dataset: Synthetic Properties: 199,998 single-turn conversations. Prompts were sampled with a fixed seed in equal shares across nine categories of Nemotron-Post-Training-Dataset-v2: STEM, code, math, chat, and multilingual German, Spanish, French, Italian and Japanese. Prompts longer than 3,072 tokens were dropped. For every response position the target’s top-20 output distribution under its serving policy was recorded, and that distribution is the training signal.

Evaluation Dataset:

Link: SPEED-Bench (qualitative and throughput-32k subsets) Data Collection Method by dataset: Hybrid: Manually-Collected, Synthetic Labeling Method by dataset: Synthetic Properties: SPEED-Bench is a unified benchmark for speculative decoding spanning coding, humanities, math, multilingual, QA, RAG, reasoning, roleplay, STEM, summarization and writing. Its qualitative subset has 1,138 conversation turns; its throughput-32k subset has 1,536 single-turn prompts of 33k to 46k tokens, split evenly across the low_entropy, mixed and high_entropy categories.

Inference:

Acceleration Engine: vLLM

Test Hardware: NVIDIA Groq 3 LPU (Language Processing Unit)

The intended deployment of these drafts is on NVIDIA Groq 3 LPU hardware, where they are compiled for the LPU and run under its runtime. The published checkpoints are standard HuggingFace models and also load in vLLM, SGLang and TRT-LLM.

Speculative Decoding with an External Draft Responses to the prompts above were synthesized by Qwen3.8-27B, and the draft was trained to match the target’s output distribution at every response position. At serving time the draft samples a block of candidate tokens autoregressively under the target’s serving policy (temperature 1.0, top-k 20, top-p 0.95), and the target verifies the block by rejection sampling against the same policy, resampling a rejected token from the residual distribution. Drafting is therefore probabilistic rather than greedy, and the accepted sequence has exactly the distribution the target would have produced on its own. The number of tokens returned per target step is the acceptance rate.

All three drafts tie their input embedding to their output head, so the embedding column counts that one matrix, which is large because the target's vocabulary is 248,320 tokens. The Llama-3.2-1B draft is bigger than the stock model for the same reason: its embedding and output layers were rebuilt on that vocabulary. The MTP head carries no embedding or output head of its own and reads the target's, which are a further 2.54B parameters it does not have to store but cannot run without.

Model Limitations:

The base model was trained on data that contains toxic language and societal biases originally crawled from the internet. Therefore, the model may amplify those biases and return toxic responses especially when prompted with toxic prompts. The model may generate answers that may be inaccurate, omit key information, or include irrelevant or redundant text producing socially unacceptable or undesirable text, even if the prompt itself does not include anything explicitly offensive.
