You have to develop a symbiotic relationship with the model. cmandres November 17, 2026, 10:34pm 16 
A symbiotic relationship would require the model to actually adapt to the feedback it receives, which is exactly what the 6.0 and 6.1 models cannot do , hence my question. 
I’ve spent years working with ChatGPT models, developing extensive writing workflows, refining instructions, establishing characterizations, and testing different approaches. I’m perfectly familiar with the collaborative aspect of working with an LLM, and have put a lot of time into making things as easy for it to use as possible. 
When a model can accurately dissect its own output, identify exactly where it deviated from the author’s intentions, explain how to correct it, and then proceed to make the exact same mistakes in its next attempt, what precisely is developing a “symbiotic relationship” supposed to accomplish then? 
The problem is the model’s apparent behavioral conditioning overriding what users desire from it. It compulsively sanitizes conflict, softens characterization, imposes emotional reassurance, prematurely resolves tension, summarizes, skips, or outright makes a total mess of physical acts, and treats the depiction of morally questionable behavior in fictional characters as something requiring correction immediately - and then it forces those corrections into the narrative without being instructed to do so. 
These issues extend well beyond explicit content and affect even relatively mundane narrative scenarios of any kind of adult topics, such as violence, substance abuse, mental health, arguments, flirtation, dangerous scenarios, even introspection. 
I’d encourage you to read the Opt-In Adult Mode thread , which currently sits at over 640 responses, if you haven’t already - and don’t let the title fool you into thinking “they just want porn.” 
Numerous experienced users like myself have documented these exact problems, compared model generations, provided concrete examples, and exhausted countless prompting approaches, and still, it is impossible to use the 6.0 or 6.1 series for any kind of narrative work if you’re writing about anything that isn’t puppies and unicorns. 
At some point, we need to acknowledge that a recurring, extensively documented model behavior is a product issue rather than a collective failure of its users to establish the correct relationship with it. 
The burden of compensating for these regressions should hardly fall on the people paying to use the tool. 
Hence my original question: have these issues actually been addressed in the new models? Because improved intelligence, reasoning, and instruction comprehension mean absolutely F### all to a fiction writer when the model continues to disregard or override the instructions it clearly understands.
