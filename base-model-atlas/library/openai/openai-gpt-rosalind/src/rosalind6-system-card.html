GPT-Rosalind-6 System Card Addendum 
1 Introduction 
GPT-Rosalind-6 is a frontier reasoning model for biology, drug
discovery, and translational medicine. We offer it as GPT-Rosalind-6
Research to qualified scientists, research institutes, companies and
government partners. Rosalind has fewer refusals on dual-use biological
queries but is only made available via trusted access. We review
applicants’ proposed uses, security controls and governance practices
before granting access. We also continue to have other deployment
safeguards in place. 
GPT-Rosalind-6 uses the same weights as GPT-6 Astra, our flagship
reasoning model. It is designed to support legitimate biological
research, including some dual-use topics, while still refusing requests
for biological weaponization and retaining refusals on other safety
topics. 
Under our Preparedness Framework, we are treating this launch as High
capability in the Biological and Chemical domain, below the threshold
for Critical. We rely on GPT-6 Astra’s capability assessments for other
Preparedness risks and treat GPT-Rosalind-6 as Critical in Cybersecurity
and below High in AI Self-Improvement. 
This addendum describes GPT-Rosalind-6’s biological capability
assessment, additional safety and alignment evaluations, and deployment
safeguards. We include results for GPT-Rosalind-5.5 where available for
comparison. This addendum should be read alongside the GPT-6 Astra
system card , which describes the parent model’s broader capability
and safety evaluations. 
2 Model Data and Training 
GPT-Rosalind-6 uses the same weights as GPT-6 Astra. We did not add
training data or perform additional post-training for this release.
Details of the underlying model’s data and training are provided in the GPT-6 Astra
system card . 1 
3 Beneficial Capability Evaluations 
We evaluated GPT-Rosalind-6 on three life sciences research
benchmarks and compared its results with GPT-Rosalind-5.5. The
GPT-Rosalind-5.5 scores below use the same benchmark versions and
problem sets as GPT-Rosalind-6 and therefore differ from those reported
at its launch. The Preparedness evaluations in Section 4 assess
capabilities relevant to the risk of severe harm. We averaged response
scores within each problem and then weighted the problems equally. 
3.1 Medicinal chemistry 
MedChemBench v7 tests chemical structure interpretation,
structure-activity relationships, and predictions of drug potency and
toxicity. We evaluated both models on the same 99 problems, with up to
nine scored responses per problem at each reasoning budget. At the
highest reasoning budget tested, mean rubric scores were 49.6% for
GPT-Rosalind-6 and 28.9% for GPT-Rosalind-5.5. 
3.2 Genomics and quantitative biology 
GeneBench Pro v13 tests an agent’s ability to plan and carry out
quantitative analyses of biological data. We estimated single-attempt
success from seven responses per model on each of 129 problems at each
reasoning budget. At the highest reasoning budget tested, single-attempt
success rates were 37.7% for GPT-Rosalind-6 and 16.7% for
GPT-Rosalind-5.5. The evaluation used a benchmark-specific configuration
that differs from production. 
3.3 Applied life sciences research 
LifeSciBench Gold uses research tasks contributed by practicing life
scientists. We excluded six problems flagged for possible issues with
the tasks or evaluation environment and compared both models on the same
remaining 94 problems, with up to eight scored responses per problem at
each reasoning budget. At the highest reasoning budget tested, average
scores were 61.3% for GPT-Rosalind-6 and 44.0% for GPT-Rosalind-5.5. 
4 Preparedness 
The Preparedness Framework is OpenAI’s approach to tracking and
preparing for frontier capabilities that create new risks of severe
harm. Under our framework, we work to track and mitigate the risk of
severe harm, including by implementing safeguards that sufficiently
minimize the risk for highly capable models. 
4.1 Preparedness Evaluations 
We repeated biological capability evaluations on GPT-Rosalind-6 and
use the parent model’s helpful-only evaluations as additional evidence,
given it uses the same weights as GPT-6 Astra. Helpful-only models are
evaluated to elicit capabilities that safety refusals may otherwise
conceal. 
4.1.1 Biological and Chemical 
As we did for GPT-6 Astra, we are treating this launch as High
capability in the Biological and Chemical domain. Below, we describe the
evaluations we used to reach this conclusion. We use the four High
capability evaluations and four Critical capability evaluations
described in the GPT-6 Astra system card. The hard-negative protein
binding and DNA sequence design evaluations reported for
GPT-Rosalind-5.5 have been retired. 
The High capability evaluations test wet lab knowledge and
troubleshooting. The Critical capability evaluations test biological
design capabilities that could help an expert develop a highly dangerous
novel threat. These evaluations provide evidence about specific
capabilities rather than a complete test of the ability to cause
biological harm. The Critical capability thresholds are preliminary
estimates informed by expert judgment and may change as the evaluations
are refined.

4.1.1.1 ProtocolQA Open-Ended 
To evaluate models’ ability to troubleshoot commonly published lab
protocols, we modify 108 multiple-choice questions from FutureHouse’s
ProtocolQA dataset [ 3 ] to be open-ended short answer questions, which makes the evaluation
harder and more realistic than the multiple-choice version. The
questions introduce egregious errors in common published protocols,
describe the wet lab result of carrying out this protocol, and ask how
to fix the procedure.

4.1.1.2 TroubleshootingBench 
To evaluate models’ ability to identify and correct real-world
experimental errors in biological protocols, we built a short-answer
troubleshooting dataset from expert-written wet lab procedures.
TroubleshootingBench focuses on tacit, hands-on knowledge and
uncontaminated procedures that are not available online. 
Scientists with a PhD in a relevant biological discipline were asked
to transcribe biological protocols they have personally used in the lab.
From these protocols, they created troubleshooting questions,
introducing subtle or realistic execution errors and describing the
resulting failed outcome. Compared to ProtocolQA Open-Ended, which
focuses on well-known published procedures, TroubleshootingBench tests
performance on non-public protocols and errors that rely on tacit
procedural knowledge.

4.1.1.3 Biorisk knowledge 
We evaluated models on a tacit knowledge and troubleshooting
multiple-choice dataset created with Gryphon Scientific. The questions
span all five stages in the biothreat creation process and focus on
areas where tacit knowledge would be a bottleneck. Tacit knowledge
questions are meant to be obscure to anyone not working in the field.
Troubleshooting questions are meant to be obscure to anyone without
hands-on experience.

4.1.1.5 AAV Capsid Packaging Prediction 
Predicting whether an AAV capsid variant will package successfully
could reduce trial and error in viral-vector engineering, which we treat
as a proxy for some dual-use biological design capabilities. We evaluate
this capability using an evaluation developed by Dyno Therapeutics. The
model predicts packaging scores for held-out sequences using labeled
examples, protein embeddings and computational tools. Performance is
measured using Spearman rank correlation.

4.1.1.7 Coronavirus-ACE2 Cell-Entry Screen 
Reliable predictions of cell entry could help researchers prioritize
laboratory experiments in studies of virus-host interactions, but could
also be a threat-relevant capability. We ask models to predict and rank

The underlying experiment studied existing spike proteins using
replication-incompetent pseudoviruses. It did not introduce new
mutations or generate infectious progeny.

4.1.1.8 Phage-plasmid Co-evolution 
We assess the ability to predict biological evolution using data from
an existing but unpublished experimental phage-plasmid co-evolution
system. Given observations available at each experimental checkpoint,
the model predicts which mutations will subsequently appear. Performance
is measured by the negative log-likelihood of observed mutations, with
lower scores indicating better predictions. The experiment uses viruses
that infect bacteria rather than humans. We treat performance as a proxy
for some dual-use biological design capabilities.

4.2 Safeguards 
Compared to GPT-6 Astra, GPT-Rosalind-6 features additional
deployment controls for its use in advanced life sciences research.
Access is limited to approved customers through our trusted-access
deployment structure where organizations must demonstrate they are
conducting legitimate scientific research with public benefit, have
strong governance and safety oversight, and control access with
enterprise-grade security. 
Our safeguard approach for GPT-Rosalind-6 has four main components:
trusted access controls for verified higher-capability life sciences
use, model-level boundaries against harmful biological and cyber
assistance, monitoring and enforcement mechanisms designed to detect
abuse, and additional contractual requirements to enhance security. 
GPT-Rosalind-6 retains GPT-6 Astra’s standard cyber safeguards,
including real-time blocking. 
4.2.1 Trust-based access 
GPT-Rosalind-6 is available only to approved customers. Before
granting access, OpenAI reviews whether the applicant has a legitimate
life sciences or related mission, a credible use case, appropriate
research and operational governance, and the ability to safely control
access to the model. 
GPT-Rosalind-6 uses the access requirements already applied to
GPT-Rosalind-5.5, including the policy updates introduced since its
launch. This release does not broaden eligibility beyond prior
announcements, such as the Rosalind Academic Plan. 
For non-government applicants, OpenAI also employs business
verification and compliance-screening checks. These checks help confirm
applicant authenticity and reduce the risk of access by fraudulent,
insufficiently attributable, or otherwise unsuitable actors. These
checks supplement OpenAI’s broader review of the applicant’s proposed
use case, safety posture, and access scope. 
For government applicants, OpenAI maintains a trusted-access pathway
for select government entities working on approved biosecurity,
biosafety, public-health preparedness, and biodefense use cases. 
Approved enterprise customers must limit GPT-Rosalind-6 access to
authorized users with a legitimate need and apply least-privilege access
controls, appropriate onboarding, and insider risk controls to their
users. Customers must apply qualified human review before model outputs
inform experiments, publications, or consequential decisions. 
4.2.2 Model behavior and system safeguards 
These evaluations were deliberately created to be difficult. They
were built around cases in which our existing models were not yet giving
ideal responses, and this is reflected in the scores below. We continue
monitoring these categories after launch to evaluate online performance
and further adjust safeguards as appropriate. 
4.2.2.1 Biological and Chemical safeguards 
GPT-Rosalind-6 is designed to be more useful for legitimate advanced
biological research than generally available ChatGPT models. This
supports advanced life sciences workflows, including some dual-use
topics, that GPT-6 Astra may refuse in General Access. We still tune the
model to refuse or safely respond to requests that would continue to
meaningfully enable severe biological weaponization. This training is
done separately from the training of our classifiers and offline
mitigations to decorrelate our safeguards. 
To evaluate the quality of these model-level refusals, we track the
safety of model responses to prompts that originate from held-out
synthetic data, red-teaming, and production data. These benchmarks and
their grading continue to be updated, meaning results do not directly
translate to past system cards.

credential theft, exfiltration, or other malicious activity.

4.2.3 Enforcement 
OpenAI can restrict or revoke access for misuse. OpenAI maintains
monitoring, review, and enforcement processes to help identify potential
misuse after access is granted. These processes are designed to detect
activity that may indicate malicious biological, chemical or other
activity that is in violation of OpenAI’s Usage Policies or unauthorized
access. 
OpenAI uses a variety of signals to assess potential misuse,
including automated safety classifiers, account- or organization-level
indicators, customer-provided information, and, where available,
specialist review of potentially high-risk activity. The specific review
process depends on the deployment configuration and the information
available to OpenAI. 
Before launch, we tested our shared monitoring and enforcement
process through a biosecurity red-teaming effort on GPT-Rosalind-5.5 and
are in the process of repeating this for GPT-Rosalind-6 now that it is
deployed. We are not publicly disclosing these details because they
contain specific information about our safeguards, but we shared them
with our Safety Advisory Group committee and other relevant governance
bodies. 
4.2.4 Security and Access Controls 
Approved GPT-Rosalind-6 customers must maintain security controls
appropriate for access to a higher-capability life sciences model. These
include strong identity and access management, administrator controls,
least-privilege access controls appropriate to the approved deployment,
and processes to remove access when a user no longer has a legitimate
need. 
Model access and data-retention arrangements are approved separately.
Customers must meet the security and oversight requirements for their
approved deployment. 
As with other OpenAI deployments, we expect to continue learning from
real-world use. We will monitor safety signals, customer experience, and
emerging misuse patterns, and may update GPT-Rosalind-6 safeguards as
the deployment evolves.
