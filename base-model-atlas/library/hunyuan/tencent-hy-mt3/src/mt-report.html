Hy-MT3: More Accurate, Robust and Practical Multilingual Translation Models in the Wild Tencent Hunyuan Team Abstract 
Hy-MT3 is the third generation of the Hunyuan multilingual translation model family, including Hy-MT3-1.8B , Hy-MT3-7B and Hy-MT3-30B-A3B (MoE) . Compared with Hy-MT2, Hy-MT3 translates more accurately, more completely and more faithfully to user constraints : on real-world scenario data, the share of samples with mistranslations is reduced by 43% , omissions or additions by 74% , and instruction-following errors by 40% , while language coverage is expanded from 38 to 102 languages.
With only 3B activated parameters, Hy-MT3-30B-A3B achieves the best overall performance among all evaluated models in non-thinking mode , including flagship models such as Qwen3.8-Max, Kimi-K3.1-Pro and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, and approaches the strongest closed-source thinking models such as GPT-6.1-Sol and Gemini-4-Argon, while surpassing models that consume more than 30 × \times its inference compute.
Beyond general translation, Hy-MT3 is optimized for two industrial scenarios. For game localization , built with high-quality game-domain data and a professional evaluation system from the Honor of Kings team, Hy-MT3-30B-A3B matches or outperforms strong closed-source models such as Gemini 3.1 Pro on all core metrics, and can be plugged into game development pipelines through the open-source toolchain TiMiHOKGameTranslator. For image translation , Hy-MT3 improves context understanding and error correction to better tolerate OCR errors, achieving the best result among models of comparable size.
Finally, we open-source TransRubric , a rubric-based automatic evaluation suite for translation, which outperforms other evaluation schemes on the WMT26 metrics shared task test set and is more stable with respect to human evaluation.

1 From Hy-MT2 to Hy-MT3 
Building on Hy-MT1.5 ( Zheng et al., 2025 ) , Hy-MT2 ( Zheng et al., 2026 ) introduced a family of fast-thinking translation models (1.8B, 7B and 30B-A3B) for real-world translation that follow translation instructions in multiple languages. As Hy-MT2 was adopted by the open-source community and in more production scenarios, several limitations became apparent. 
First, faithfulness remains the main source of errors in real-world translation . A fine-grained analysis of Hy-MT2-30B-A3B on 5,000 samples of real-world scenario data (Table 5 ) shows that 56.0% of its outputs contain at least one accuracy issue and 21.2% contain omissions or additions. Such errors are rarely captured by surface-level fluency, but they directly determine whether a translation can be used without post-editing. 
Second, language coverage is insufficient for long-tail demand . Hy-MT2 covers 38 languages and performs poorly on lower-resource languages outside this set: on 64 additional lower-resource languages paired with Chinese or English, Hy-MT2-30B-A3B obtains a TransRubric score of only 63.89 (ZH ⇔ \Leftrightarrow XX) and 67.75 (EN ⇔ \Leftrightarrow XX). 
Third, vertical scenarios impose requirements that general benchmarks do not measure . Game localization requires strict preservation of placeholders and rich-text tags as well as consistent use of in-game terminology, and a single off-target or broken tag can break the game client. Image translation takes OCR output as input, which often contains misrecognized characters, missing text or wrong segmentation that the translation model must tolerate and correct from context. 
Finally, reliable automatic evaluation becomes the bottleneck . As strong models converge on standard benchmarks, holistic scores and neural metrics ( Guerreiro et al., 2023 ; Rei et al., 2022 ) struggle to distinguish them or to explain where they fail. We need an evaluation method that is fine-grained, interpretable and consistent with human judgments, both to measure progress and to guide model development. 
To address these issues, we present Hy-MT3. Our main contributions are as follows: 
• 
More accurate, complete and constraint-faithful translation. Compared with Hy-MT2-30B-A3B, the share of samples with mistranslations, omissions/additions and instruction-following errors is reduced by 43%, 74% and 40%, respectively, and language coverage is expanded from 38 to 102 languages (Appendix A ). 
• 
Outperforming much larger models with high efficiency. Hy-MT3-30B-A3B achieves the best overall performance among all evaluated models in non-thinking mode, including Qwen3.8-Max, Kimi-K3.1-Pro and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, and approaches the strongest closed-source thinking models such as GPT-6.1-Sol and Gemini-4-Argon. 
• 
Game translation. Together with the Honor of Kings team, we improve game translation with high-quality game-domain data and a professional evaluation system. Hy-MT3-30B-A3B matches or outperforms strong closed-source models such as Gemini 3.1 Pro on all core metrics, and an open-source toolchain allows it to be integrated into game development pipelines for high-quality, low-cost localization. 
• 
Image translation. Hy-MT3 is optimized for OCR-cascaded image translation, with stronger context understanding and error correction to tolerate OCR errors, achieving the best result among models of comparable size. 
• 
TransRubric. We open-source TransRubric, an automatic evaluation suite for translation, which outperforms other evaluation schemes on the WMT26 metrics shared task test set and is more stable with respect to human evaluation. 
2 TransRubric: Rubric-Based Automatic Evaluation for Translation 
To evaluate translation quality in a fine-grained and interpretable way, we develop TransRubric, a rubric-based LLM-as-a-judge ( Zheng et al., 2023 ; Kocmi & Federmann, 2023 ) evaluation suite, and use it as the primary metric throughout this report. Given a source text, a candidate translation, the translation instruction and optionally a reference translation, a TransRubric judge checks the candidate against a translation rubric, identifies each issue together with its error type (e.g., completeness, accuracy, terminology and instruction following) and severity level, in the spirit of MQM-style error annotation ( Lommel et al., 2014 ; Freitag et al., 2021 ) , and outputs a pointwise score on a 0–100 scale. The issue annotations make the score interpretable and enable error analyses such as Table 5 . TransRubric supports both reference-based evaluation (used for FLORES-200, WMT26, Mandarin ⇔ \Leftrightarrow Minority and HardMTBench) and reference-free evaluation (used for WildMTBench-202611, which has no reference translations). 
In this report, each TransRubric score is the arithmetic mean of two judges, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash ( DeepSeek-AI, 2026 ) and Qwen3.5-397B-A17B ( Qwen Team, 2026a ) . To make scores comparable across models, each benchmark score is computed with the fixed expected number of samples as the denominator, so empty outputs and unscored samples count as 0. 
Meta-evaluation on WMT26. We validate TransRubric on Task 2 (segment-level quality score prediction) of the WMT26 shared task on automated translation quality evaluation ( Lavie et al., 2026 ) , following its official meta-evaluation on the eight priority translation directions: screen-based Pairwise Difference Pearson (PDP) at the segment level, source-based Soft Pairwise Accuracy (SPA) at the system level, and their average (Avg Corr). In this experiment, TransRubric runs reference-free with Gemini 3.6 Flash as the underlying judge. As shown in Table 1 , TransRubric achieves the highest PDP (0.744) and Avg Corr (0.832), ahead of the top-ranked official submission Gemini-3.6-Flash (0.736 / 0.830), which uses the same underlying model, as well as GPT-5.6 Sol (0.828) and the ensemble system bytepop_pro10 (0.826). Its system-level SPA (0.920) is on par with the best system (0.924), and all LLM-based evaluators clearly outperform conventional metrics such as chrF, COMET22 and xCOMET. Comparison with human annotators. We further compare TransRubric with 10 professional annotators majoring in English on 651 translations, i.e., 217 source texts with three candidate translations each, covering both Chinese → \rightarrow English and English → \rightarrow Chinese. Each annotator scores every translation once on a 0–100 scale, and TransRubric (with the DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and Qwen3.5-397B-A17B judges) scores every translation 10 times. Figure 2 summarizes the results. 
• 
Stability. For each translation, we compute the standard deviation of its 10 scores. Across the 10 human annotators, the per-sample standard deviation averages 15.57 points, whereas across the 10 TransRubric runs it averages only 3.31 points (average variance 285.79 vs. 26.20). TransRubric scores are thus far more consistent than human judgments. 
• 
Pairwise accuracy. For the three translations of each source text, we form all pairs (651 pairs in total) and check whether each rater prefers the same translation as the gold standard. The gold score of each translation is obtained by English translation experts, who revise the 10 annotators’ scores and take the average of the revised scores. Since the gold standard is built on the annotators’ own scores, this comparison may favor the human annotators. TransRubric reaches 59.91%, ranking 4th among the 11 raters: it is higher than 7 of the 10 annotators and lower only than three of them (61.29%, 60.37% and 60.06%), well above the human average of 54.7%. 3 Experiments 
3.1 Experimental Setup 
Benchmarks. We evaluate Hy-MT3 from three perspectives. 
• 
General translation. FLORES-200 ( Team et al., 2022 ) on 33 languages with all 33 × 32 = 1056 33\times 32=1056 directions; FLORES-200 on 64 additional lower-resource languages paired with Chinese or English (256 directions); WMT26 GenMT ( Kocmi et al., 2026 ) on 20 language pairs after excluding English → \rightarrow Ligurian, English → \rightarrow Ladin and English → \rightarrow Northern Sami; and Mandarin ⇔ \Leftrightarrow Minority translation between Mandarin and Tibetan, Uyghur, Traditional Mongolian, Arabic-script Kazakh and Cantonese (10 directions). 
• 
Real-world translation. WildMTBench-202611, the latest version of our in-the-wild benchmark, consists of 5,000 samples of real-world scenario data evenly distributed over 10 domains; HardMTBench ( Li et al., 2026b ) is an open-source benchmark of knowledge-intensive, long-form Chinese--English cases in both directions 1 1 1 https://github.com/jasonNLP/HardMTBench . 
• 
Translation instruction following. IFMTBench ( Zheng et al., 2026 ) 2 2 2 https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hy-MT2/tree/main/IFMTBench contains 7,344 samples covering industrial translation constraints such as code tags, inline code, layout preservation, style, glossary, context and structured data, split into single-constraint (Simple) and multi-constraint (Complex) subsets. 
In addition, we report human evaluation on game translation (Section 3.7 ) and cascaded image translation on VISTA-Bench ( Lv et al., 2026 ) (Section 3.8 ). 
Metrics. We report TransRubric (Section 2 ) as the primary metric, and GEMBA ( Kocmi & Federmann, 2023 ) with GPT-OSS-120B ( OpenAI, 2025 ) (medium reasoning) as a complementary LLM-based metric. IFMTBench is scored with its official rules and LLM judge. 
Baselines. We compare Hy-MT3 with Hy-MT2 and three groups of baselines: (1) general models in non-thinking mode, including Kimi-K3.1-Pro ( Moonshot AI, 2026 ) , DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash ( DeepSeek-AI, 2026 ) , the Qwen3.8 ( Qwen Team, 2026c ; Qwen Team, 2026d ) / Qwen3.6 ( Qwen Team, 2026b ) series and the Gemma4 series ( Gemma Team, 2026 ) ; (2) translation-specialized models and commercial translation APIs, including the Index-Translate series ( Li et al., 2026a ) , Tower-Plus-72B ( Rei et al., 2025 ) , Qwen-MT-Flash ( Qwen Team, 2025 ) , Microsoft Translator, Volcano Translator and iFlytek Translator; and (3) models in thinking mode, including GPT-6.1-Sol ( OpenAI, 2026 ) , Claude-Opus-5.5 ( Anthropic, 2026 ) , Gemini-4-Argon ( Google DeepMind, 2026 ) , GLM-5.3 ( GLM-5 Team, 2026 ; Z.ai, 2026 ) and the thinking variants of the open-weight models above. All Hy-MT3 models are run in non-thinking mode, so the most direct comparison is with groups (1) and (2).

3.7 Game Translation

Together with the Honor of Kings team, we collected high-quality game-domain data to make Hy-MT3-30B-A3B a leading model for game translation. We also jointly built an evaluation system covering multiple game genres. The evaluation set is stratified by localization characteristics such as text length, rich text and placeholders, and includes long texts with high terminology density from MOBA and open-world games, to comprehensively test how well a model handles game context, terminology and complex text structures. Under this system, we establish four game-specific evaluation criteria: off-target rate, placeholder errors, rich-text errors and terminology errors.

3.9 Quantization 
To support deployment under different resource budgets, we evaluate Hy-MT3 under several quantization settings: FP8, and the Q8, Q4_K_M and IQ2 formats of llama.cpp, ranging from 8-bit to 2-bit precision. Table 8 compares them with the BF16 models. 
8-bit and 4-bit quantization is nearly lossless for the larger models. For Hy-MT3-7B, FP8, Q8 and Q4_K_M stay within 0.6 points of BF16 on every benchmark; for Hy-MT3-30B-A3B, the gap is within 0.4 points, and some settings even score slightly higher than BF16. 
2-bit quantization remains usable. With IQ2, the scores of Hy-MT3-7B and Hy-MT3-30B-A3B drop by at most 1.95 and 2.53 points, respectively, with the largest drops on low-resource FLORES-200, WMT26 and Mandarin ⇔ \Leftrightarrow Minority translation. 4 Conclusion 
We present Hy-MT3, a family of multilingual translation models comprising Hy-MT3-1.8B, Hy-MT3-7B and Hy-MT3-30B-A3B. Compared with Hy-MT2, Hy-MT3 substantially reduces mistranslations, omissions/additions and instruction-following errors, and expands language coverage from 38 to 102 languages. Running in non-thinking mode with only 3B activated parameters, Hy-MT3-30B-A3B achieves the best overall performance among all evaluated non-thinking models on general translation, real-world translation and translation instruction following, and approaches the strongest closed-source thinking models at a fraction of their inference compute. Hy-MT3 further delivers leading results in game localization and OCR-cascaded image translation. Together with the models, we release TransRubric to support fine-grained and reliable evaluation of translation quality. We hope Hy-MT3 and TransRubric will facilitate both research and real-world applications of machine translation.
