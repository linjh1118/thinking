OmniEVIE: Evidence-Vector-Informed Embedding for Multi-modal Retrieval Zifei Wang 1,∗ Wei Wen 2,∗† Qiang Ji 1 Qian-Wen Zhang 1,2 Ruizhi Qiao 2 He Liu 1 Xing Sun 2 Qiang Lin 2 Affiliation: 1 Tencent IMA Product Center 2 Tencent Youtu Lab November 19, 2026 Abstract 
Multimodal retrieval now has to match dense pages, fine-grained entities, ordered video, and the tools and memory an agent looks up. A single vector drops the local evidence that decides these matches. A cross-encoder can recover it, but only by rereading every recalled page or clip. Late interaction keeps the evidence in the index; storing every token vector is too large to serve. OmniEVIE is a 4.5B multi-vector embedding model that covers text, images, visual documents, video, and tools with one compressed index and a three-stage retriever. Each record holds at most 128 128 int8 vectors of dimension 64 64 : 9,472 9{,}472 bytes per record, or 8.82 8.82 GiB per million records. Retrieval runs on that compressed index, and a returned hit names the page region or the video interval. Training, merging, indexing, and retrieval are designed together. Every task is written as one retrieval sample. A three-grade evidence check removes false negatives, and the sampling rate follows the gap between the baseline and the teachers. Candidates are compressed with ST-HAC during training, in the form stored in the index, so the training score and the serving score use the same vectors. Stage-wise runs and specialist branches are merged by parameter block. A saliency gate and a noise floor rewrite the query score. One compressed record supplies a mean vector, 1 1 -bit signs, and int8 multi-vectors, used for the coarse, middle, and final stages. Trained on public datasets, the full sequence at d = 2048 d=2048 scores 60.68 60.68 on MMEB-V3 (190 tasks), and the K ​ 128 × d ​ 64 K128\times d64 int8 index scores 60.06 60.06 . The same compressed index scores 77.96 77.96 on MMEB-V2 (78 tasks) and 62.96 62.96 on ViDoRe V3 (48 tasks). On corpora of 1 1 million and 5 5 million records, one single-thread query takes 54.2 54.2 ms and 120.2 120.2 ms, 11.1 × 11.1\times and 25.3 × 25.3\times faster than exact search over 4096 4096 -dimensional single vectors, and 236 × 236\times and 532 × 532\times faster than full int8 MaxSim. Video is encoded directly from frames and a hit is read back to its start and end in seconds, so neither indexing nor localization uses a video parsing model. On the 1,083 1{,}083 queries of the QVHighlights validation set, the heat peak falls inside the labeled segment for 78.0 % 78.0\% of them and the returned span intersects it for 94.3 % 94.3\% , against 44.9 % 44.9\% for a random span of the same length; the span covers 18 % 18\% of the video on average. 1 Introduction 
1.1 From a single vector to multi-vector retrieval 
Retrieval queries now include multi-page reports, fine-grained products and landmarks, long videos with ordered actions, and agent contexts built from tool descriptions, interface state, and multi-step history [ 12 , 22 , 9 ] . Relevance often depends on a few non-adjacent pieces of evidence: a header and one cell in a financial table, the order of two actions in a video, or one parameter in a long tool document. 
A single d d -dimensional vector scores a query and a candidate by one inner product. Weller et al. [36] show that this score can place only some combinations first: past a corpus size that grows polynomially with d d , a document that must be recognized by several pieces at once cannot be ranked first by any query. Adding data or enlarging the backbone does not remove that limit. 
Late interaction keeps a query token set Q = { q 1 , … , q L q } Q=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{L_{q}}\} and a candidate set D = { v 1 , … , v K } D=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{K}\} , and scores each query token by its best match in the candidate [ 13 , 6 ] : 
MaxSim ⁡ ( Q , D ) = ∑ i = 1 L q w i ​ max 1 ≤ j ≤ K ​ ⟨ q i , v j ⟩ , \operatorname{MaxSim}(Q,D)=\sum_{i=1}^{L_{q}}w_{i}\max_{1\leq j\leq K}\langle q_{i},v_{j}\rangle, (1) 
with w i > 0 w_{i}>0 . Because each query token can land on a different span, the score is no longer limited by the dimension of one vector. The leading visual-document and text-to-video retrievers use this form [ 6 , 23 , 27 , 33 ] . 
1.2 Three obstacles at serving time 
That representation is expensive to serve, for three separate reasons. A high-resolution page or a clip of tens of seconds produces thousands of vectors, so a million records occupy hundreds of gigabytes in floating point. A query then has to be compared with every vector: one single-thread query on a million-record corpus takes on the order of ten seconds. Failure modes also differ across documents, images, video, and tools: upweighting one modality often degrades the others, and a model trained on the full token sequence loses accuracy once those tokens are compressed for the index. 
1.3 What OmniEVIE does 
The usual compromise retrieves with one vector and reranks with a cross-encoder [ 23 , 17 ] . Each of the k k recalled pages or clips is forwarded again with the query, so the system has to read the raw media online. An uncompressed multi-vector index has the opposite problem: prior studies show that MaxSim selects only about 1 % 1\% of the stored vectors [ 25 ] . OmniEVIE keeps the width at d ≤ 128 d\leq 128 , bounds the number of vectors, and stores them at low bit width. Search runs on that compressed index (Table 1 ). 
The model extends the evidence check and the local multi-vector index of EVIE from visual documents to general multimodal retrieval [ 34 ] . A mined candidate stays a negative only when it lacks the evidence the query asks for. Position codes guide clustering and are then discarded, so the index stores semantic centroids. The same integer record is what the retriever filters and what it uses to name the hit. This release is trained on 57 57 public datasets. The changes are in the backbone, the order in which modalities are added, and the decision to train on the same clustered vectors that are written to the index. 
A hit also carries the place of its evidence. A page or an image returns the bounding rectangle of the hit clusters, and a video returns the start and end in seconds of the hit centroids, so the downstream generator reads that region or that span instead of the whole page or video. The video side encodes the sampled frames directly and takes time from the frame indices, so neither indexing nor localization goes through a parsing model that transcribes video into text. 2 Related work 
2.1 General multimodal embeddings and multi-vector representations 
One general embedding model is now expected to handle text, images, documents, and video. VLM2Vec and the MMEB suites put retrieval, classification, question answering, and grounding into one evaluation [ 12 , 22 , 9 ] . Esfandiarpoor et al. [5] measure what that similarity score costs once the query needs several pieces of evidence. Qwen3-VL-Embedding, WeMM-Embedding, Ovis-Embedding, and U-MARVEL train a single vector with multi-stage contrastive learning, hard negatives, reranker supervision, and cross-scale distillation [ 17 , 40 , 24 , 19 ] . By contrast, jina-embeddings-v4 and v5-omni keep both a single vector and a multi-vector output, and lock the vision tower so the geometry stays aligned [ 7 , 8 ] . 
Late interaction was established for text by ColBERT and ColBERTv2 [ 13 , 30 ] and carried to full-page visual documents by ColPali, which replaces an OCR pipeline with a page screenshot [ 6 ] . ViDoRe V3 extends that evaluation to multilingual pages [ 20 ] . Jina-ColBERT-v2 covers multilingual text [ 11 ] . ModernVBERT and Nemotron-ColEmbed-V2 study bidirectional attention and model merging for document late interaction [ 32 , 23 ] . Video-ColBERT and CLAMR extend late interaction to video [ 27 , 33 ] . EVIE applies evidence checking, a Prefix-MRL head, and layout-aware HAC to visual documents [ 34 ] . Matryoshka representation learning and MatFormer nest several widths in one forward pass [ 15 , 3 ] . MetaEmbed changes the number of meta-tokens at test time [ 37 ] . OmniEVIE carries this design to images, video, text, and agent tasks, and adds a shared spatiotemporal compression, block-wise merging, query-side scoring, and million-scale three-stage retrieval. 
2.2 Compression and fast multi-vector search 
Compressing a multi-vector index is a choice of which part of the match to keep. MUVERA replaces MaxSim by one fixed-dimensional vector so that an existing maximum-inner-product index can be reused [ 4 ] . PLAID keeps the multi-vector score but filters through a global codebook and residual codes [ 29 ] , and query-aware token budgeting delays the choice of raw tokens until the final stage [ 28 ] . A third line reduces the number of stored vectors, either by merging tokens inside the encoder [ 1 ] or by clustering and pruning a frozen index, as in attention-guided clustering [ 25 ] , MarginMerge [ 21 ] , and prune-then-merge [ 39 ] . ST-HAC belongs to this third line, with one difference: the same clustering is used while training and again when the index is written. The mean, the 1 1 -bit signs, and the int8 multi-vectors are then three readings of that one record, at a fixed int8 scale. 
2.3 False negatives, rank distillation, and model merging 
The quality of a contrastive embedding depends on which negatives are false and on whose ranking is distilled. FALCON studies false negatives in large-scale image-text pretraining [ 14 ] . CORE distills a reranker ranking into an embedding with Rank-KL [ 31 ] . On the parameter side, task arithmetic adds and subtracts task vectors [ 10 ] , and TIES-Merging drops small deltas and resolves sign conflicts [ 38 ] . Conan-embedding-v3, PlaM, and training-free mitigation study modality-specific fusion, layer-selective injection, and the recovery of language ability after multimodal tuning [ 18 , 35 , 26 ] . The merge in OmniEVIE gives each parameter block its own coefficient, so the merger and the projection head can take a different step from the language layers. 
3 Training data 
OmniEVIE puts every task into one retrieval sample. The instruction says which task it is, the evidence grade says whether a mined negative is truly negative, and a dynamic sampling rule adjusts how often each sample is revisited.

3.3 Evidence grades and temporal hard negatives 
High-scoring mined candidates are the usual source of hard negatives, and they are also where missed positives hide. If a page that answers the query is treated as a negative, the gradient pushes that page away from the query. A multimodal judge assigns each mined negative one of three grades (Table 3 ). 3.4 Dynamic sampling from a baseline and several teachers 
Not every sample deserves the same update. Some are already solved by the baseline, and on some tasks every available teacher is weak. The teacher is chosen by the form of the answer. Open answers and fine-grained classification use a multimodal embedding teacher and its in-batch similarity distribution. Ranking tasks, including text-to-image, video grounding, and tool retrieval, use a cross-encoder and the gaps among candidates. Multiple choice uses a discriminative teacher and the distribution over options. 
Each sample is then labeled by whether the baseline and the teacher place the positive first (Table 4 ). Samples that both models rank correctly by a wide margin are downsampled. Samples the teacher ranks correctly and the baseline misses are upweighted. When the baseline beats the teacher, distillation is turned off and the loss uses the gold label. If the baseline outperforms the teacher on more samples of a task than the reverse, distillation is turned off for that task. 4 Model and training 
4.1 Backbone, bidirectional attention, and native video 
OmniEVIE is built on the Qwen3.5-VL mixed-attention backbone: a vision encoder, a cross-modal merger, language layers, and a Prefix-MRL head. The released model has 4.5 4.5 B parameters and hidden width d hid = 2560 d_{\mathrm{hid}}=2560 . Language layers alternate GatedDeltaNet blocks and full attention. The document stage distills an 8 8 B bidirectional EVIE teacher with d hid = 4096 d_{\mathrm{hid}}=4096 into this 4.5 4.5 B model [ 34 ] . 
Text is embedded by the tokenizer. Images and pages become patch tokens after the vision encoder and a spatial merge. A causal mask would hide a later instruction from the visual tokens that precede it. Full-attention layers use bidirectional attention, and the mask marks padding. Linear-attention blocks stay as in the backbone. Every token can then attend over the full sequence [ 23 , 34 , 32 ] . 
OmniEVIE feeds each clip as one sequence, so adjacent frames meet inside the encoder. Per-frame encoding instead runs one forward per frame and pools the vectors afterward, so frames meet only after the backbone, a reversed clip yields the same set of frames, and exceeding the token budget forces dropping frames. Adjacent frames are packed at the vision-encoder input with temporal stride Δ ​ t patch = 2 \Delta t_{\mathrm{patch}}=2 , and the last frame is repeated when the count is odd. Each temporal group is prefixed by a timestamp in seconds. When the token budget is exceeded, every frame is scaled down by the same factor and the time samples stay. Timestamps increase with position, so a reversed clip pairs content with a different timestamp, and the reversed negatives of Section 3 have a nonzero gradient.

4.5 Query-side saliency gate and noise floor 
Uniform MaxSim, with w i = 1 w_{i}=1 on every non-pad token, fails on multimodal queries for two reasons. Visual patches and background tokens dominate the sum, and every query token can find a medium inner product on any candidate. Those medium matches add up and dilute the real hit.

6 ST-HAC 
6.1 Agglomerative clustering 
A high-resolution page or a 32 32 -frame clip emits hundreds to thousands of vectors, and they are not spread uniformly. The vectors that Section 1.3 counts as unselected are mostly collinear background and static frames [ 25 ] . The evidence that decides a retrieval, a single cell, a small logo, or a short action, is an isolated point far from the background. The compression merges collinear background and static frames into a few clusters, and leaves an isolated piece of evidence in a cluster of its own. Ward clustering merges the pair whose union increases the within-cluster sum of squares the least: nearby background tokens merge first, and an isolated point stays until the end. ST-HAC runs this clustering in a spatiotemporal feature space. Table 13 compares it with uniform pooling and random pruning.

9.6 Limitations 
A clip of a few seconds, sampled at a low frame rate, collapses to a handful of temporal groups under the stride of 2 2 , so little of the action remains. The frame rate can be chosen from the length of the clip. Reasoning retrieval, GUI, and memory are still the lowest text and agent subgroups; they need multi-step reasoning retrieval data and interface screenshots. The gate and the noise floor are computed inside S3, so their cost grows linearly with L q L_{q} . 
10 Conclusion 
OmniEVIE serves text, images, documents, video, and tools from one 4.5 4.5 B model and one index. MMEB-V3 is 60.06 60.06 , 0.62 0.62 below the full d = 2048 d=2048 sequence at 60.68 60.68 on the same 190 tasks. A record is at most 9,472 9{,}472 bytes, about 8.8 8.8 GiB per million records. On one million and five million records, a single thread takes 54.2 54.2 ms and 120.2 120.2 ms, 11.1 × 11.1\times and 25.3 × 25.3\times faster than exact search over a 4096 4096 -dimensional vector. Search uses the compressed index. The hit names a page region or a video interval. 
Training and search use the same score. Evidence grades decide which negatives are false. Candidates are clustered during training into the vectors that will be stored. Specialists trained separately are merged by parameter block, and each block receives its own coefficient. Online, the score is an inner product, a popcount, or an int8 dot product. 
An agent that retrieves several times in one task benefits when each hit already names its evidence: the generator then reads that region or interval (Figure 9 ). This release tests the architecture, the schedule, and the compressed index on public data: 57 57 datasets and about 2 2 M samples. The next stage adds domain data, extends the same record to audio and to long mixed documents, and passes the localized region or interval to a generator. At larger scale the mean vector can itself be quantized or placed in a graph index. 
Weights are at https://huggingface.co/tencent/OmniEVIE-4B . Inference, indexing, and the three-stage retriever are at https://github.com/Tencent/OmniEVIE . The corpora are public benchmarks and licensed document, image, and video collections. The evidence check filters sensitive material and personally identifiable information.
