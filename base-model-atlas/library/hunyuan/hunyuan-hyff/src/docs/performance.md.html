tf32x3 evaluates fp32 matrix products as three TF32 tensor-core products (error comparable to fp32). fp32 statistics compute the LayerNorm mean and variance in fp32 at force time; fp64 computes them in fp64. Learned weights are never cast. Override with precision=, statistics= (--precision, --statistics); engine='reference' runs the plain PyTorch model.

The flexible drift falls with the square of the timestep, as expected for velocity Verlet; moderato, andante and largo show the same behaviour at 0.5 fs (−4 to −7 meV/atom/ns over 1–2 ps). Rigid water at 1 fs is the default; flexible water needs 0.5 fs or less.

The MD step itself is unchanged. A move costs one force evaluation, one or two neighbour-list rebuilds and some host work, so its share falls for larger systems and models.

Checks: an in-place cell change gives the energy and forces of an engine built for the new cell (|ΔE| ≤ 1 × 10⁻⁵ eV, |ΔF| ≤ 2 × 10⁻⁶ eV/Å for all five models; for largo also on a 20,688-atom box whose SPME mesh sum is computed). The pressure from energy differences under molecular scaling, a route independent of the barostat, averages +24 ± 80 bar over a 1 bar presto NPT run and −1,507 ± 54 bar in NVT at the starting density (1.006 g/cm³), from which NPT compresses the box. For groups without interactions the sampled volume follows the ideal-gas NPT distribution in all three coupling modes (unit test).

Tips

  • Use one Python process per simulation; each process may construct at most two compiled engines.
  • Kernel compilation happens at the first force call and is cached on disk (Triton and Inductor caches),

so a second run on the same system starts faster.

  • chunk (steps per device launch, default 100) amortizes launch overhead for the native engines;

larger values reduce thermostat and output frequency.

  • A larger skin reduces neighbour-list rebuilds at the cost of more neighbours per atom.
  • For many small molecules use hyff.predict_batch rather than one engine per molecule.
  • Multi-GPU MD only pays off for very large largo systems: at 103,440 atoms (1 fs NVE, chunk 100) one H20 takes

6,347 ms/step, four 2,207 and eight 1,173; at 2,586 atoms two GPUs (301 ms/step) are slower than one (121). See largo.md.
