Code World Model 2.0: Towards Infinite Controllable Video Generation Ruoyu Wang 1,2,∗ Hao Zhong 1,3,∗ Yanming Yang 1,2,∗ Jiahao Tian 1,2,∗ Lin Liu 1,∗,† Biao Gong 1 Guoshan Lu 1 Yiding Tian 1 Chunhua Shen 3,‡ Zhenzhong Lan 1,2,‡ Chi Zhang 2,‡ Affiliation: 1 Inclusion AI, Ant Group 2 Westlake University 3 Zhejiang University ∗ Equal contribution † Project leader ‡ Corresponding author Abstract 
Visual proxies decouple explicit world-state simulation from photorealistic video synthesis, offering a controllable interface for both offline clip creation and real-time interactive streaming. Yet a practical renderer must address two challenges. Upstream proxies vary from coarse layouts to fine articulated geometry depending on the interaction, and streaming inference must operate in a few causal steps without accumulating drift across self-generated history. We present Invoker-Render , pairing a bidirectional creation model with a causal four-step streaming model under a shared conditioning interface over structural proxies, an appearance reference, and language. Trained on an aligned ∼ \sim 200-hour multi-granularity corpus, Invoker-Render honors fine geometric constraints when supplied while synthesizing plausible local motion under coarse proxies, enabling runtime selection of input detail. To bridge bidirectional creation and real-time streaming without compounding step compression and rollout drift, we introduce Progressive Forcing (ProForcing) . ProForcing first trains a causal predictor and distills it into a four-step renderer using histories derived from training videos. Training then shifts to continuous self-generated rollouts. A pipelined serving stack supports efficient streaming inference. Experiments show versatile control across proxy families, granularities, and camera paths, consistent gains over recent streaming baselines, and stable 30-minute racing rollouts that preserve scene and subject appearance.

1 Introduction 
Interactive video world models must synthesize coherent observations as user actions alter the environment [ 2 , 52 , 70 , 51 ] . Rather than simulating both dynamics and appearance in one network, an emerging paradigm maintains explicit state in an upstream engine and delegates visual synthesis to a generative renderer [ 8 , 20 , 56 ] . Visual and geometric proxies bridge the two stages by prescribing camera paths, layout, and entity motion while leaving photorealistic appearance to the video model [ 27 , 41 , 7 , 26 ] . Recent systems pair such structural cues with appearance references for offline authoring and real-time rendering [ 12 , 34 , 31 , 19 , 67 ] . 
In practice, the structural detail appropriate for conditioning depends on the interaction. Broad navigation often requires only coarse scene geometry and root trajectories, whereas close-range interactions demand articulated poses or finer surface structure. Detailed proxies supply tighter constraints at higher construction and transmission cost, while coarse proxies are lightweight to produce yet leave local geometry and motion under-specified. A capable renderer must interpret both regimes faithfully. For example, a rigid mannequin that tracks a character’s root pose should constrain global position and heading without imposing a frozen limb posture on the generated motion. 
We introduce Invoker-Render , a proxy-conditioned rendering framework comprising a bidirectional creation model and a causal four-step streaming model . Both accept structural proxies, an appearance reference, and language through a shared interface. Training covers different proxy families, such as semantic layouts and shaded geometry, and multiple granularities within a family. Each checkpoint can follow fine constraints when supplied and synthesize plausible local motion when omitted, supporting different input granularities with the same weights. 
For video creation, users edit complete proxy sequences to refine camera paths, trajectories, and event timing. The bidirectional model attends across the full clip. During streaming, future states depend on actions yet to occur. The causal model therefore consumes proxy chunks and local text incrementally, generating each chunk in four steps using retained visual history. 
We build on MiniMax-H3 [ 42 ] and adopt Guidance-Free Training (GFT) [ 6 ] to adapt its guidance-distilled backbone to proxy conditioning. Converting the resulting bidirectional model to a streaming renderer requires both fewer denoising steps and tolerance to errors in generated history. Progressive Forcing (ProForcing) addresses these changes in stages (Figure 4 ). Teacher forcing (TF) first trains block-causal prediction on clean video prefixes. Resampling forcing (RF) then constructs imperfect histories by adding noise to observed prefixes and refining them sequentially with the current model. 
RF-DMD retains these resampled histories while learning four-step generation through distribution matching distillation (DMD) [ 64 , 63 ] and an auxiliary GAN loss [ 23 ] . The student, trainable score model, and GAN discriminator then continue training on continuous self-generated histories in SF-DMD [ 18 , 61 , 14 ] . Long-rollout training thus starts with a four-step generator and supervision models already trained alongside it. 
We train on an aligned ∼ \sim 200-hour multi-granularity corpus with hierarchical text. Persistent history caching and pipelined proxy encoding and video decoding reduce serving overhead. Across 140 streaming rollouts, Invoker-Render outperforms recent streaming baselines in visual quality, structural adherence, and reference preservation, and maintains stable scene and subject appearance across 30-minute racing rollouts. 
Our contributions are: 
• 
Mixed-granularity proxy conditioning. A shared conditioning interface lets each creation or streaming checkpoint accept coarse and fine proxies. 
• 
Progressive Forcing. A curriculum progresses from TF/RF causal adaptation to four-step DMD with GAN supervision, then continues the trained student, score model, and discriminator under self-generated histories. 
• 
Long-horizon controllable generation. Our four-step causal renderer outperforms recent streaming baselines and maintains scene and subject appearance across 30-minute racing rollouts. 
• 
Multi-granularity corpus and serving stack. An aligned ∼ \sim 200-hour dataset and a low-latency distributed serving engine support multi-granularity training and continuous interactive streaming. 
2 Related Work 
2.1 Interactive Worlds and Generative Rendering 
Action-conditioned video models often internalize world dynamics and visual synthesis in one network, learning latent or discrete action transitions from gameplay and video corpora [ 2 , 52 , 10 , 4 , 54 , 57 , 70 , 40 ] . LingBot-Video studies mixture-of-experts video pretraining for embodied intelligence, combining web videos with robot-oriented footage [ 39 ] . Streaming architectures extend interactive horizons across open-world interactions and explicit context or 3D-aware memory, including the Matrix-Game series, WorldPlay, WorldCrafter, and related streaming world models [ 16 , 53 , 44 , 68 , 51 , 66 , 46 , 13 , 62 ] . Because one visual transition model governs both state evolution and rendering, modifying scene structure or enforcing persistent physical rules remains challenging. 
A complementary paradigm maintains explicit state in an executable engine or agentic controller and delegates synthesis to a conditioned video model. Code World Model, Programmable World Model, and AgentGarten compile structured program state into visual signals [ 8 , 20 , 9 ] , Marionette decouples state prediction, geometry rendering, and appearance painting [ 41 ] , and Wizard supports real-time world manipulation via agentic control [ 55 ] . StreamRender-H3, Streaming CWM, Magpie, and BoxWorld likewise couple game or simulator backends with neural renderers through semantic, white-box, or bounding-box streams [ 34 , 28 , 67 , 56 ] . Treating proxies as an explicit state-to-renderer contract, Invoker-Render studies controllable granularity across creation and streaming. 
2.2 Proxy-Conditioned Video Generation 
Building on spatial control, camera conditioning, and unified video creation frameworks [ 69 , 15 , 22 ] , proxy-conditioned video models ground synthesis in geometric or semantic structure across several regimes. Paired methods learn directly from dense geometry, lighting, material buffers, or animated meshes, as in DiffusionRenderer, Generative World Renderer, and native 4D mesh rendering [ 26 , 21 , 7 ] . Proxy2World relaxes paired supervision via posed RGB-D clips [ 59 ] , whereas training-free methods such as Code Video Model restrict proxy injection to early denoising steps so appearance follows the reference image [ 27 ] . 
Recent renderers broaden representation diversity, customization, and structural fidelity. SolarRenderer reuses the backbone’s reference pathway across heterogeneous structural videos with a four-step acceleration LoRA [ 12 ] , EdenLive and Mundo support real-time reference-driven rendering and customized world synthesis [ 19 , 36 ] , Fight World Model controls combat interactions via visual proxies [ 25 ] , and AnyGameRenderer preserves cross-game entity counts and timing [ 31 ] . Invoker-Render targets an orthogonal axis: structural granularity within one representation family, enabling a single model to honor fine constraints when supplied and synthesize natural local motion under lightweight coarse proxies. 
2.3 Streaming Video Diffusion and Few-Step Distillation 
While training-free queue scheduling such as FIFO-Diffusion enables continuous generation via diagonal denoising [ 24 ] , interactive streaming requires block-causal attention and few-step sampling. Diffusion Forcing trains causal models with independent token noise levels [ 5 ] , CausVid distills a bidirectional teacher into an autoregressive student [ 65 ] , and Self Forcing rolls out the student on its own predictions [ 18 ] . LongLive stabilizes rollouts via streaming tuning and a persistent frame sink [ 61 , 58 ] , SANA-WM and minWM scale minute-horizon and full-stack world modeling [ 77 , 71 ] , and AlayaRenderer-Flash and StreamRender-H3 co-design few-step causal rendering with low-latency serving [ 29 , 34 ] . 
To compress flow-matching trajectories [ 30 , 32 ] , consistency distillation maps states directly to clean targets [ 49 ] , score-based distribution matching [ 35 , 64 , 75 ] aligns distributions via auxiliary score differences, and DMD2, ADD, and LADD pair score or diffusion distillation with discriminator supervision [ 63 , 48 , 47 ] . Multi-step variants refine supervision across steps, with Trajectory Distribution Matching (TDM) and VA-TDM aligning multi-step trajectories and velocity fields [ 37 , 38 ] and Endpoint Distribution Matching (eDMD) anchoring the fake score model on final clean outputs [ 73 ] . For causal video, Causal Forcing uses an autoregressive ODE teacher, Causal Forcing++ and Causal-rCM adopt consistency-based causal initialization [ 78 , 72 , 74 ] , and Salt++ aligns streaming generation and scoring contexts [ 14 ] . ProForcing instead distills a four-step student with teacher-feature relativistic GAN supervision [ 23 , 47 ] on resampled data-anchored histories (RF-DMD) before continuing the coupled student, fake score model, discriminator, and optimizer states on continuous self-generated rollouts (SF-DMD). 
3 Data Curation and Proxy Construction 
Controllable rendering requires aligned training pairs that cleanly separate the geometry prescribed by a proxy from the appearance and local dynamics synthesized by the model. Our corpus comprises approximately 200 hours of footage, combining 150 hours of self-collected data with 50 hours from public datasets, and associates each usable RGB clip with structural conditions, an appearance reference, and hierarchical text annotations. We design the pipeline around three core requirements: spatial-temporal correspondence between proxies and RGB targets, multi-granularity structural representations of the same event, and language descriptions tailored to both whole-window creation and block-causal streaming.

3.2 Aligned Scene States and Multi-Granularity Proxies 
Figure 3 outlines capture and proxy construction for our self-collected corpus. While recorded RGB serves as the supervision target, synchronized engine buffers and reconstructed 3D scene states (comprising camera intrinsics and extrinsics, static environment geometry, and tracked dynamic entities) allow us to render multiple structural granularities of the exact same visual event. Figure 3: Capture and multi-granularity proxy construction pipeline. From a shared scene state, the pipeline renders matched coarse and fine structural proxies for each event. Capture and reconstruction. 
Starting from synchronized RGB and engine depth capture, our reconstruction pipeline recovers camera calibration, scene semantics, and 3D entity motion. Instance segmentation and multi-object tracking [ 45 ] isolate moving actors and vehicles from the static scene, which is labeled via semantic segmentation and completed across invalid depth regions [ 60 ] , while camera pose estimation and background point tracking recover globally consistent trajectories. For dynamic actors, human motion recovery estimates both global root trajectories and articulated body poses, and 3D bounding-box fitting tracks rigid vehicle motion. Retaining per-frame camera parameters, track IDs, and confidence masks alongside the video enables automated reprojection and tracking verification prior to proxy rendering. Coarse structural control. 
Coarse proxies encode global scene layout, camera motion, and entity placement and heading while omitting fine local geometry. Humans are rendered as canonical rigid mannequins driven solely by root pose, vehicles as oriented 3D boxes, and backgrounds as simplified geometry or semantic regions. Because a canonical mannequin conveys root translation and orientation without limb articulation, the paired RGB target and action text teach the generator to synthesize natural gait and posture consistent with the trajectory. Fine geometric and motion control. 
Fine proxies augment this representation with articulated human poses (rendered as depth-tested volumetric body parts) and shaded local surface geometry, while preserving rigid vehicle boxes. Wherever both variants are constructed, coarse and fine proxies share identical camera parameters, timestamps, entity identities, and RGB targets, directly supervising the renderer across constraint strengths for the same event. Throughout this work, we distinguish granularity , meaning the level of geometric and kinematic detail within a representation, from representation family , such as semantic maps, shaded depth, or white-box renders. Separating structure from appearance. 
To prevent appearance leakage, both granularities strip away surface textures, materials, and scene lighting. Before compositing dynamic entities, foreground masks are excised from background depth and semantic buffers and inpainted from temporally adjacent background views, suppressing silhouette cues from clothing, hair, or accessories. Reprojecting the simplified dynamic primitives onto this cleaned background with depth occlusion ensures that proxies convey pure spatial and motion structure, leaving appearance to the reference frame, language, and learned video prior. 
3.3 Sequence Lengths and Hierarchical Language Short windows and continuous sequences. 
Self-collected and public recordings are standardized at 24 fps into 158-frame windows ( ∼ \sim 6.6 s) and 328-frame continuous sequences ( ∼ \sim 13.7 s). RGB targets use 1344 × 768 1344\times 768 , while aligned proxies are stored at 672 × 384 672\times 384 and encoded at 672 × 384 672\times 384 or 448 × 256 448\times 256 using nearest-neighbor resizing for semantic maps and anti-aliased filtering for continuous geometry. Short windows support bidirectional GFT, supervised TF/RF, and RF-DMD, whereas 328-frame sequences support variable-length SF-DMD rollouts across uninterrupted trajectories. Whole-window descriptions. 
Text annotations combine clip-level scene descriptions with timestamped local actions (“Scene + local actions” in Figure 3 ). Each window pairs its opening RGB anchor with a vision-language caption covering environment, subject appearance, lighting, events, and camera motion. The bidirectional creation model and the bidirectional teacher and fake score models each receive independent whole-window captions rather than concatenated local tags. Streaming action annotations. 
Timestamped local annotations track action progression, motion directions, interactions, and camera shifts across block boundaries so that TF/RF and the distilled student observe text strictly up to the current block, without future labels or clip summaries. For coarse proxies, local text disambiguates behaviors whose articulation is omitted from rigid root geometry. Condition organization. 
A system instruction defines the roles of structural proxies and the appearance reference alongside sample captions, while automated validation checks reference tags, temporal order, and entity-action agreement. 
3.4 Quality Control and Evaluation Separation 
Quality control screens both video and proxies for corrupted or frozen frames, exposure failures, shot cuts, reprojection outliers, tracking switches, and timing drift. Automated geometric checks and manual review evaluate each proxy against its intended constraints, distinguishing omitted coarse articulation from root-trajectory or camera errors. 
Records track source identifiers, frame ranges, proxy variants, captions, and encoding provenance, with cross-source deduplication across shared game worlds. Training and held-out splits are partitioned by recording (and by scene or map where identifiable), keeping overlapping windows, coarse/fine pairs, and alternative views of one recording in the same split to prevent leakage. 
4 Invoker-Render 
Invoker-Render adapts MiniMax-H3 [ 42 ] into both a bidirectional video creation model and a causal four-step streaming renderer. We first formalize the shared conditioning interface and linear flow parameterization, then present bidirectional proxy adaptation via Guidance-Free Training, and finally detail Progressive Forcing (ProForcing), which distills few-step causal rendering on data-anchored histories before transitioning to continuous self-generated rollouts.

4.4 Long-Sequence Self-Forcing 
Building on self-forcing and streaming long tuning [ 18 , 61 ] , SF-DMD replaces data-anchored RF prefixes with continuous student-generated histories. Starting from the appearance anchor a a , the student sequentially denoises each block in four steps and commits its detached terminal prediction 𝒙 ^ 0 b \widehat{\bm{x}}_{0}^{b} to the causal history cache ( 𝒉 SF b = 𝒙 ^ 0 < b \bm{h}_{\mathrm{SF}}^{b}=\widehat{\bm{x}}_{0}^{<b} ). While detached terminal outputs advance the rollout state, selected step predictions 𝒙 0 θ \bm{x}_{0}^{\theta} retain local computation graphs for DMD and GAN supervision. Student weights remain fixed throughout the multi-block rollout, accumulate gradients across scored intervals, and take a single consolidated update before the next rollout refreshes its history from scratch. Local scoring of a continuous stream. 
To supervise rollouts longer than the bidirectional teacher’s native window, we tile the continuous student stream with overlapping local scoring windows. Each window pairs preceding generated blocks (serving purely as detached temporal context) with a disjoint interval of newly generated target blocks, always retaining the original appearance anchor a a . The teacher, fake score model, and discriminator observe complete conditions within each local window while the student remains causal, and DMD and GAN input gradients are masked to apply strictly once per non-anchor target block, weighted by target coverage. Coordinated continuation from RF-DMD. 
Crucially, transitioning from RF-DMD to SF-DMD carries forward the entire coupled training state (student weights, fake score model 𝒇 fake \bm{f}_{\mathrm{fake}} , discriminator D ϕ D_{\phi} , their respective AdamW moments [ 33 ] , and the student EMA) against the same frozen teacher. Because RF-DMD already converges a capable four-step causal generator together with calibrated supervision models tailored to its output distribution, SF-DMD avoids cold-start critic instability and focuses optimization on mitigating long-horizon error accumulation under self-generated histories. 
5 Streaming Serving Infrastructure 
To deploy the causal world model as an online interactive rendering service, each streaming step must encode incoming visual proxies, update retained clean history H H and current proxy conditioning C C within a bounded KV window, run four-step causal denoising, and return decoded RGB frames within a strict latency budget.
Starting from a standard streaming baseline, we streamline the inference stack across three levels:
(i) an asymmetric streaming codec pairing a lightweight Tiny AutoEncoder (TAE) for online proxy encoding with a causal video decoder for RGB reconstruction;
(ii) intra-window invariant reuse and fused execution across DiT layers, KV workspaces, and prefill/denoising passes; and
(iii) cross-packet codec overlap and asynchronous CPU delivery (Figure 5 ).
On an eight-GPU node at 1344 × 768 1344\times 768 resolution, these optimizations reduce the steady-state per-chunk period from 1770 ms to 930 ms and increase throughput from 9.9 FPS to 18.0 FPS ( 1.82 × \times speedup; Figure 6 ). 5.1 Efficient Asymmetric Streaming Codec 
Interactive streaming places asymmetric demands on the visual codec: incoming proxy frames must be encoded at every chunk with minimal latency, whereas generated latents must be decoded into temporally continuous RGB video.
Encoding online proxies with the full video encoder is slower than output decoding and dominates the inter-chunk codec interval. 
We therefore adopt an asymmetric codec design.
The static reference frame is encoded once at session start with the full encoder to preserve anchor detail, while online proxy packets are encoded independently by a lightweight TAE proxy encoder directly into normalized 16 × 16\times spatially compressed, 24-channel conditioning latents.
On the output side, we retain the tensor-parallel Causal Video Decoder to preserve temporal continuity across chunks.
Replacing the online proxy encoder with TAE makes input encoding an order of magnitude faster than causal decoding ( T enc ≪ T dec T_{\mathrm{enc}}\ll T_{\mathrm{dec}} ), reducing the steady-state chunk period from 1770 ms to 1410 ms and raising throughput from 9.9 FPS to 12.4 FPS . 
5.2 Intra-Window Reuse and Fused Execution 
Within each DiT window, we eliminate redundant per-layer preparation, projection compute, memory allocation, and separate prefill launches: 
• 
Cross-layer structural sharing. Within a single forward pass, all DiT layers share identical token layout plans, sliding-window crop indices, and frame-aligned RoPE position tensors.
We construct these plans and index tensors once at backbone entry and reuse them across all layers. 
• 
Per-layer AdaLN projection caching. Because session conditioning is stationary and the four-step Euler timestep schedule repeats across chunks, we cache each DiT block’s projected AdaLN modulation parameters by timestep and reuse them across subsequent chunks. 
• 
Transactional KV workspace reuse. Instead of allocating fresh output and concatenation buffers at every layer, we reuse pre-allocated KV result backplanes and temporary forward workspaces across the transaction lifecycle (Figure 5 , top).
Combining structural invariant and workspace reuse reduces the steady-state chunk period from 1410 ms to 1180 ms and lifts throughput from 12.4 FPS to 14.4 FPS . 
• 
Fused prefill-and-denoise pass ( H/C/D 1 \text{H/C/D}_{1} ). In ordinary continuation windows, we defer committing prior clean history H n − 1 H_{n-1} and pack it with incoming proxy tokens C n C_{n} and the first denoising state D 1 D_{1} into a single block-causal composite forward (Pass 1 in Figure 5 ), followed by three denoising-only passes ( D 2 D_{2} – D 4 D_{4} ).
Reducing backbone launches from six to four trims the chunk period from 1180 ms to 1165 ms and raises throughput to 14.8 FPS . 
5.3 Cross-Packet Pipelining and RGB Delivery 
With intra-window DiT execution compressed, we pipeline codec execution and host frame delivery across packet boundaries to remove inter-chunk idle gaps: 
• 
Cross-packet codec overlap. Between consecutive DiT windows, causal video decoding for chunk n n and TAE proxy encoding for chunk n + 1 n{+}1 run concurrently on disjoint GPU subsets (Figure 5 , center and bottom).
Because TAE encoding finishes well within the causal decoding window, next-packet proxy encoding is hidden behind current-packet decoding. 
• 
Pipelined CPU delivery. Once causal decoding and the device-to-host copy into shared RGB memory complete, frame packing and socket emission are offloaded to an asynchronous CPU worker.
The GPU pool immediately starts chunk n + 1 n{+}1 ’s DiT passes while host delivery proceeds in parallel, cutting the steady-state chunk period from 1165 ms to 930 ms and reaching 18.0 FPS . 7 Conclusion 
We presented Invoker-Render, a proxy-conditioned video rendering framework that unifies bidirectional video creation and four-step causal streaming under a shared multi-granularity interface. Built on a ∼ \sim 200-hour multi-granularity video-proxy corpus, Progressive Forcing decouples causal adaptation and four-step DMD and GAN distillation on data-anchored histories from stateful continuation on self-generated rollouts, mitigating long-horizon drift while preserving structural fidelity and reference identity. Co-designing this renderer with a fused, KV-persistent, and codec-pipelined serving stack enables low-latency chunked execution, yielding consistent gains in visual quality, structural tracking, and reference preservation across streaming evaluations and sustaining coherent 30-minute racing rollouts toward infinite controllable generation. 
Several directions remain open for scaling proxy-conditioned world rendering. First, although our static prefix sink and sliding KV window bound inference cost and sustain appearance over extended sessions, visual details outside the active window are eventually evicted once they leave the local context. Incorporating explicit long-term memory mechanisms directly into causal training and distillation, such as learnable memory compression, spatial-temporal context retrieval, or persistent 3D-aware state caches, would allow the renderer to recall previously visited environments and maintain multi-entity appearance consistency across revisits. Second, because current training uses a fixed proxy granularity within each rollout window, supporting dynamic mid-stream level-of-detail transitions and coupling Invoker-Render with interactive state engines in closed loop remain promising avenues for future work.
