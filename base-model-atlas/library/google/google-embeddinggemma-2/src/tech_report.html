We present EmbeddingGemma 2, an open multimodal embedding model which natively represents text, images, audio, video, and combinations thereof in a unified space. Our approach introduces improvements such as simplified per-layer embeddings and a multi-stage curriculum with modality and objective progression which ensure strong quality across unimodal, cross-modal, and mixed-modal evaluations. Notably, in comprehensive embedding benchmarks across all modalities, EmbeddingGemma 2 establishes a new Pareto frontier, outperforming both specialist models and models two to twelve times larger. Furthermore, our design offers flexibility for resource-constrained environments, supporting selective modality encoder loading, adjustable vision token budgets, truncatable embedding dimensions, and quantized weights. We present ablations analyzing key decisions and release EmbeddingGemma 2 to promote further research. 
1 Introduction 
Embedding models map data to fixed-length vector representations, placing semantically similar data near each other in the embedding space. Text embedding models have become a popular choice for tasks like information retrieval, semantic similarity, clustering, and retrieval-augmented generation. However, to work with audio and visual data, these require additional tools like automatic speech recognition (ASR) and image captioners which both add computational cost and fail to capture signals like fine-grained visual details and non-verbal or environmental sounds. 
Multimodal embedding models like CLIP [ 1 ] , ALIGN [ 2 ] , and SigLIP [ 3 ] add a modality tower for vision, and so achieve strong cross-modal quality, but they are unable to natively process mixed-modal input to capture interactions between input modalities. Recent efforts have adapted Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) into embedding models that allow interleaved mixed-modal input and support more complex tasks such as visual question answering and visual grounding [ 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 ] ; subsequent work has built on more capable MLLMs to incorporate video and audio modalities [ 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 ] . However, as MLLMs have grown in size, so have embedding models adapted from them. These models typically contain several billion parameters, making them prohibitively expensive for applications that are latency-sensitive, high-throughput, or require on-device deployment for privacy or offline access. 
In this paper, we introduce EmbeddingGemma 2, 1 1 1 Our model is available at https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/embeddinggemma . a 744M-parameter general-purpose multimodal embedding model that maps text, images, video, and audio—as well as arbitrary interleaved combinations thereof—into a single unified embedding space ( Figure 1 ). EmbeddingGemma 2 combines a 271M-parameter bidirectional transformer backbone (embedder included) with a 167M-parameter vision encoder and a 305M-parameter audio encoder. The modality encoders independently convert media into sequences of soft token embeddings that enter the shared transformer backbone, which means these parameters are modular: EmbeddingGemma 2 can alternatively be run with 271M parameters as a text-only model, with 439M as a vision-language model, or with 577M as an audio-language model. Figure 1 : EmbeddingGemma 2 natively maps text, images, video, audio, and combinations thereof into a unified, semantically-aligned vector space. 
The model builds on the architecture and weights from the capable Gemma 4 model family [ 13 ] and learns from the powerful Gemini Embedding 2 model [ 8 ] . Our training regime consists of three stages spanning various tasks, modalities, and domains, which results in a broadly capable model, excelling at problems like code search, visual question answering, video moment retrieval, audio classification, and much more. We separate text-only and multimodal training as a form of curriculum learning, and separate distillation from contrastive and regularization objectives in order to effectively learn an embedding space tailored to the model. For extreme resource-constrained settings, the model offers high-quality truncated embeddings and separate checkpoints adapted with quantization-aware training. 
We present ablation studies explaining our architectural and training choices. We find that simplifying per-layer embeddings to omit the dedicated lookup table not only reduces cost but improves performance; we present the effects of using different vision token budgets during training and inference, observing it only significantly affects tasks like document retrieval which require fine-grained visual understanding; we demonstrate the importance of co-training the vision encoder; we show the impact of finetuning with contrastive learning rather than distillation; and we confirm the value of native audio understanding over ASR. Finally, we evaluate the model on seven major, comprehensive benchmark suites spanning text, code, image, video, document, speech, and sound inputs. EmbeddingGemma 2 establishes a new Pareto frontier across benchmarks spanning all modalities ( Figure 2 ). Despite using fewer parameters for text than its predecessor and handling more modalities, it improves on both multilingual text and code benchmarks. On multimodal evals, EmbeddingGemma 2 often matches or exceeds the quality of specialized and multi-billion-parameter models.

2.4 Recipe Initialization 
EmbeddingGemma 2 builds directly on Gemma 4’s extensive world knowledge and capabilities. The backbone is adapted from a pretrained Gemma 4 decoder-only model, switching the causal attention mask to bidirectional attention to enable fully contextual token representations [ 34 , 35 , 36 , 8 ] . We omit explicit adaptation stages for bidirectional attention [ 23 , 37 ] , which can be costly and have been shown to be unnecessary for learning expressive representations [ 23 , 38 ] . We also remove the PLE-specific token lookup table (detailed in Table 2 ), and add mean pooling [ 39 , 23 ] , a linear projection, and ℓ 2 \ell_{2} -normalization after the transformer. The vision encoder is initialized with the smaller Gemma 4 vision encoder and further trained in the two multimodal stages, which we show in Section 3 to be critical for quality. The audio encoder is bit-identical to that in the Gemma 4 E2B and E4B models [ 13 ] and kept frozen during training. 
We then train EmbeddingGemma 2 on a mixture of embedding tasks spanning various modalities and task types. In total, the model sees approximately 2.9T tokens, of which ∼ \sim 300B are seen during text pre-finetuning, ∼ \sim 2T during multimodal pre-finetuning, and ∼ \sim 600B during multimodal finetuning. Text Pre-finetuning 
We begin with a pre-finetuning stage like the one in Schechter Vera et al. [23] . This entails a large-scale unsupervised data mixture of query-target pairs from question answering, sentence similarity, code retrieval, and web search tasks using various languages. As before, we opt for a larger batch size both to stabilize the gradient and to provide more (in-batch) negatives for each input example. In this stage we almost exclusively employ distillation loss, with only an additional contrastive loss using a small weight. Here, as with subsequent stages, tasks are sampled at pre-determined, empirically defined rates. We found the inclusion of a text-only stage important for the model’s final text quality. This stage can be seen as a form of curriculum learning, or as an implicit warm-up for the bidirectional attention, similar to BehnamGhader et al. [37] ; it has also been shown to enhance the discriminability of both text and non-text embeddings [ 10 ] . Multimodal Pre-finetuning 
We then continue with pre-finetuning, but with a new mixture which also includes inputs with images, audio, video, and documents across unimodal, cross-modal, and mixed-modal tasks, most with additional hard negatives. The text tasks in this stage and the next include those described in the finetuning stage of Schechter Vera et al. [23] , which aim to cover task diversity, language diversity, and coding capability. In this stage, we exclusively employ distillation loss, to inherit the teacher’s ability to map diverse and mixed modalities into a shared semantic space. We continue training with larger batch sizes and larger learning rates, but train for twice as many steps as in text pre-finetuning to cover the expanded multimodal training set. Multimodal Finetuning 
In the finetuning stage, we completely replace distillation with contrastive loss. This allows the model to learn a tailored embedding space independent of its teacher, as the teacher’s embedding space is not optimized for the student’s architecture, size, and MRL sub-dimensions. We also add other losses which aim to shape the final embedding space to have desirable properties: spread-out regularizer and MRL losses. As in Schechter Vera et al. [23] , we transition to smaller learning rates and batch sizes, and shift towards higher-quality, task-specific datasets, such as tool retrieval, audio captioning, video ranking, medical imaging, visual product search, cropped-object retrieval, and academic question-answering. Model Merging 
Finally, we combine checkpoints from our finetuning run by averaging their weights, to improve our final model’s quality and robustness. Rather than running multiple finetuning jobs, either with different data mixture rates [ 23 ] or with different hyperparameters [ 40 ] , we merge checkpoints along a single finetuning trajectory [ 41 ] . Specifically, we compute an unweighted average between the final checkpoint from the finetuning run and a significantly earlier checkpoint from the same run. Like Wortsman et al. [42] , which similarly merges pretrained and finetuned checkpoints, we found that this helps mitigate degradation on tasks that overfit during finetuning, while also further improving performance on tasks that already benefited from finetuning. 4.1 Methods 
We select as baselines the top-ranked models on each benchmark, including both modality specialists and general-purpose embedding models which support all evaluated modalities. For MTEB, MAEB, and MIEB(lite), we compare against models under 1B parameters; for MMEB-v2, we include models under 2B parameters, as small models are scarce. [49] , our reproduced score is slightly higher. All other baseline scores are sourced from public leaderboards. We restrict our analysis to single-vector models, given the disparity in storage and latency costs with multi-vector models, and omit models trained on > > 25% of the evaluation data, as data contamination undermines the evaluation’s validity. 
We evaluate EmbeddingGemma 2 using half-precision weights (bfloat16) and the seven types of task instructions detailed in Appendix A . We use different sequence lengths depending on the modality and task type. For visual inputs, we use up to N frm = 32 N_{\text{frm}}=32 frames per video at 140 vision tokens per frame and 280 vision tokens per image to reflect realistic model use, though we note that higher vision token budgets could yield further gains, particularly for visual documents ( Table 5 ). Because most baseline models support limited modalities, we count only the parameters EmbeddingGemma 2 utilized for each benchmark; other models’ parameter counts are as reported on respective leaderboards.

While EmbeddingGemma 2 does beat specialists like CLAP at non-speech tasks like clustering vehicle sounds ( VehicleSoundClustering ), these models still outperform in tasks like audio captioning ( ClothoT2ARetrieval ). In speech tasks, EmbeddingGemma 2 sets the state of the art even in tasks with multiple languages ( FleursT2ARetrieval ) and singing ( JamAltLyricA2TRetrieval ). But it sometimes struggles at zero-shot tasks with a paralinguistic focus, despite understanding the acoustics deep down. For example, it struggles tremendously at clustering speakers by gender ( VoxPopuliGenderClustering : 0.40), but recovers quickly when doing the same task with a linear probe ( IEMOCAPGender : 66.40). This indicates the model’s speech representations are dominated by semantics, desirably avoiding biases such as speaker demographic while retaining paralinguistic and acoustic attributes for extraction when needed. 
The model’s excellence on spoken language is further underscored on MSEB. Even when using ASR and text embeddings, EmbeddingGemma 2 greatly outperforms EmbeddingGemma 1, indicating the strength persists throughout modalities ( Table 11 ). Encoding audio natively yields another even larger improvement. Instead of suffering from transcription errors from accents, homophones, or artifacts, native audio inputs preserve these signals and let the model take them into account. As a result, even the 256-dimensional embeddings greatly outperform the full-dimension ASR embeddings.

5 Future Work 
Our work points to several concrete directions for future research. EmbeddingGemma 2 supports long sequences and variable vision token budgets, but further extending context length, for example via content-aware methods, could enable direct indexing of full-length media, improve performance on long-horizon tasks, and support novel agentic memory systems. Innovations such as encoder-free architectures could enable both quality improvements and further reductions in memory footprint and latency. Changes in training paradigms like the introduction of end-to-end RAG training present another opportunity to further optimize embedding models for downstream use. As interleaved all-to-all multimodal search becomes more prevalent, evaluation frameworks that represent novel use cases will become more important. 
At the same time, EmbeddingGemma 2’s utility extends far beyond single-vector retrieval, serving as a general-purpose multimodal encoder. Operating on its final embeddings or unpooled token representations, the model can be adapted into (or may even excel zero-shot as) a general decision model [ 61 , 62 , 63 , 64 ] , model router [ 65 ] , reward model [ 66 ] , reranker [ 67 ] , speech-to-text model [ 68 , 69 ] , late interaction retriever [ 70 , 71 , 72 ] , and much more. 
6 Conclusion 
EmbeddingGemma 2 builds on its predecessor with a smaller, stronger backbone and native multimodal capabilities. Our approach introduces improvements like simplified per-layer embeddings and a multi-stage curriculum with both modality and objective progression. This enables EmbeddingGemma 2 to set a new Pareto frontier across text, vision, and audio benchmarks, often surpassing specialist and general-purpose models several times larger. With native support for weight quantization, embedding truncation, and modular encoder loading, EmbeddingGemma 2 makes high-quality multimodal embeddings practical in resource-constrained and on-device settings. We release EmbeddingGemma 2 checkpoints to the community to accelerate research and development, for both off-the-shelf use and as a foundational multimodal encoder.
