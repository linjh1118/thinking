GenFirst

Generation Before Reconstruction for Stable End-to-End Latent Generative Modeling.

  • Paper: arXiv:2608.29335
  • Code: github.com/ByteDance-Seed/GenFirst
  • Project page: tom-zgt.github.io/GenFirst

These weights are released under CC BY-NC 4.0: non-commercial research use only, which is stricter than the MIT licence on the code. See Licence and third-party terms before use.

What this is

GenFirst trains a latent VAE jointly with a generative prior without the latent collapse that usually makes such end-to-end training unstable. The method is a two-phase schedule over the prior and entropy weights: phase 1 lets the prior shape the latent geometry, phase 2 drops both weights tenfold so the decoder recovers detail. The result is a latent space that a diffusion prior fits well while remaining a good autoencoder.

This release covers the EiT (diffusion-prior) instantiation. The autoregressive EAR instantiation from the paper is not released.

Two kinds of artifact are published, and they are used together:

  1. End-to-end VAEs — the encoder/decoder produced by the end-to-end stage,

each shipped with its latent normalization statistics.

  1. Generative priors — SiT models for class-conditional generation, and

MMDiT models for text-to-image, each trained on a frozen VAE from (1).

Usage

These checkpoints need the training repository; there is no diffusers integration and no from_pretrained entry point.

bash
git clone https://github.com/ByteDance-Seed/GenFirst
cd GenFirst
pip install -r requirements.txt          # install torch first

Class-conditional sampling, writing a small batch of images rather than a full evaluation run:

bash
cd Prior_only
python generate.py \
    --model SiT-XL/1 --resolution 256 \
    --ckpt /path/to/prior.pt \
    --vae-arch f16d32 \
    --vae-ckpt /path/to/e2e-f16d32.pt \
    --latents-stats /path/to/e2e-f16d32-latents-stats.pt \
    --num-fid-samples 64 --label-sampling random \
    --cfg-scale 1.5 --sample-dir samples

--model must match the VAE: SiT-XL/2 for f8d4, SiT-XL/1 for f16d32. Text-to-image sampling, the guidance settings that reproduce the numbers above, and the full evaluation harness are documented in the repository under docs/.

Evaluation

Reported metrics and their protocols:

  • gFID / IS — 50,000 generated samples scored with

torch-fidelity against the reference statistics used by MAR, at the stated resolution. Sampling is SDE with 250 steps. Guided rows use autoguidance (pointing away from an early checkpoint of the same run) with CFG on top, except the 512 rows, which use plain CFG.

  • rFID / PSNR / SSIM / LPIPS — posterior-mean reconstructions over a

50,000-image validation split, following latent-diffusion. rFID here is measured against those validation images, not a training-set reference, so it is not comparable to numbers computed the other way.

  • GenEval / DPG-Bench — overall scores, Euler sampling with 50 steps and

CFG 7.0 on the interval [0.35, 1.0], no prompt rewriting.

Numbers were produced with the versions pinned in environment.yml. FID is sensitive to reference statistics and to the resizing path, so comparisons against other work are only meaningful under the same protocol.

Training

Training proceeds in two stages. The end-to-end stage trains the VAE jointly with a prior under the two-phase GenFirst schedule (global batch 256; 400k steps at the high weight, then 200k at the low one). The prior-only stage then freezes the VAE, caches its latents, and trains from scratch the prior that is actually evaluated (4M steps at 256, 2M at 512). An optional 10k-step decoder-only finetune between the stages improves unguided gFID by roughly 0.2. Text-to-image follows the same structure, with an MMDiT-L/2 prior shaping the latent during the end-to-end stage and the released MMDiT-XL/2 trained on the frozen result.

Training data consists of publicly available datasets. Individual corpora are not enumerated here; each is subject to its own licence and terms of use, which govern independently of the licence on these weights.

Intended use

Intended for research: reproducing the paper, studying end-to-end VAE and prior training, latent-space analysis, and use as a baseline or initialization for further non-commercial research.

Out of scope. Any commercial use. Generating content about real, identifiable people. Generating sexual, violent, hateful, or otherwise harmful imagery. Any use presenting outputs as real photographs or as factual records. Any safety-critical or decision-making pipeline. These models produce synthetic images with no factual grounding and must not be treated as a source of truth.

No safety filter, watermarking, or content classifier is included. Anyone deploying these weights in a setting where outputs reach other people is responsible for adding those controls.

Limitations and biases

  • The class-conditional models generate from a fixed 1000-class label set and

cannot follow text. Their outputs reflect the visual distribution and the category taxonomy they were fit to, including the geographic and cultural skew documented for that style of curated image collection.

  • The text-to-image models inherit the biases of both their training data and

the frozen CLIP-L text encoder. Expect uneven representation across demographics, professions, and cultures, and expect prompt-following to degrade on long, compositional, or counting-heavy prompts. GenEval and DPG-Bench scores measure prompt alignment on those benchmarks only and do not certify safety or fairness.

  • Text rendering, hands, and fine structure remain unreliable, as with

comparable models at this scale.

  • Metrics reported here are distribution-level. A good FID says nothing about

the quality or appropriateness of any single sample.

  • The 512 class-conditional models are reported with guidance only; their

unguided behaviour is untuned.

  • A VAE paired with mismatched latent statistics fails silently in the sense

that it produces output rather than an error. Check the pairing.

Security

A .pt checkpoint is a pickle: loading one executes arbitrary Python. These checkpoints store an args namespace alongside the tensors, so they cannot be loaded with weights_only=True and require weights_only=False on torch 2.6 and newer. Load checkpoints only from sources you trust, and verify you are downloading from the official repository.

To report a security vulnerability, follow SECURITY.md in the code repository: contact the ByteDance security team rather than opening a public issue.
