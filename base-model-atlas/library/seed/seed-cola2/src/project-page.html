Cola 2 : Co-Adaptive Hierarchical Language Modeling with
      Continuous Semantic Compression and Balanced Discrete Generation 
Compress semantics continuously, realize language discretely, and let both sides co-adapt. Hongcan Guo 1,2,‡ , Yian Zhao 2,3 , Feng Wang 2 , Shen Nie 4 , Guoqiang Wei 2,‡ , Yan Zeng 2,†,‡ , Hengshuang Zhao 1,✉ 1 The University of Hong Kong 2 ByteDance Seed 3 Peking University 4 Renmin University of China † Project Lead · ‡ Major Contributor · ✉ Corresponding author ★ Seedance Team Research Project ★ Paper New arXiv New Code New 🤗 Model New BibTeX Your browser does not support embedded MP4 video. Cola2 project overview. Download video Abstract 
Co-adapting continuous semantics and discrete generation 
Hierarchical language generation separates continuous semantic modeling from discrete text
        realization, but its effectiveness depends on what information the latent space should
        compress and how the discrete generator should use it. We propose Cola2 , which jointly
        studies continuous semantic compression, the discrete reconstruction–generation balance, and
        their co-adaptation. Cola2 dynamically segments text into semantic spans, controls the
        compression rate with an auxiliary-loss-free negative-feedback mechanism, and combines
        reconstruction and generation supervision to learn compact and predictive latent
        representations. 
A block-causal masked diffusion model (MDM) decoder is jointly trained under clean, noisy,
        and dropped latent conditions, and classifier-free guidance (CFG) adjusts the balance between
        semantic reconstruction and autonomous generation. During training, token-prediction gradients
        update the encoder through the latents, driving the continuous representation and discrete
        generation to co-adapt, while Flow Matching prior learning is decoupled from representation
        learning via stop-gradient. At inference, the generated text is re-encoded under the fixed
        span layout, keeping the latent history consistent with the actual text context. 
Systematic component analyses and controlled comparisons on five downstream tasks with about 210B training tokens validate the core designs and show competitive, unsaturated
        scaling. With only 346.03B source tokens , the unconverged checkpoint reaches a 60.10 eight-task average and surpasses Llama-3-8B on HellaSwag and StoryCloze despite
        its over 43× larger reported pretraining budget. 
These results indicate effective transfer of the Qwen-initialized causal decoder's generation
        capability and substantial scaling headroom for hierarchical language generation. More
        broadly, continuous-latent hierarchical modeling can combine effectively with established
        token-space generation paradigms, including autoregressive and masked-diffusion decoders. Method 
The Cola2 workflow 
A compressed continuous latent prior organizes semantics; a capable token-space decoder
        reconstructs those semantics and generates the remaining detail. Method overview from the paper. Stage 1 jointly trains the compression encoder and
        reconstruction–generation balanced decoder. Stage 2 additionally trains a DiT on detached
        latents. At inference, the DiT generates latents block by block and the decoder realizes them
        as tokens. Stage 1 Semantic compression 
Dynamic segmentation assigns latent positions to content-aware spans. A gradient-free controller keeps the mean compression rate near its target without competing with representation learning. Stage 2 Detached latent prior 
A block-causal DiT fits the online latent distribution with Flow Matching. Its gradient is stopped at the encoder, while token-side gradients continue to shape the representation. Inference Propose → decode → re-encode 
The DiT proposes a latent block, the decoder realizes tokens, and fixed-span feedback re-encodes those tokens so latent and discrete histories remain aligned. Experiments 
Three questions across the hierarchy 
We study how to construct a compressed semantic latent space, how to train its continuous
        generator, and how the discrete decoder should balance reconstruction with generation. 1 · How do we build a compressed, predictive semantic latent space? Main finding. Dynamic semantic segmentation and generation-oriented supervision are
          both essential. At 2× compression, the best dynamic configuration reaches 29.4% on LAMBADA
          and slightly surpasses the uncompressed model; co-evolving the encoder and decoder with
          generative supervision reaches 58.2%. Segmentation and rate control. Content-aware spans beat fixed and random grouping; auxiliary-free feedback beats DLCM. VAE architecture. Layer allocation matters more than depth; intra-span bidirectional attention is best. Capacity. Latent width has an interior optimum and maximum span length is non-monotonic. Span distribution. Larger caps reduce forcibly truncated semantic spans. Generative supervision. Predictive objectives and encoder–decoder co-evolution are complementary. 2 · How should the continuous latent generator be trained? Main finding. Keep adapting the VAE with token-side supervision, but detach the
          Flow Matching gradient from the encoder. A moderate latent block size is best, the noise
          schedule must match the representation, and inference saturates at roughly eight DiT steps. Update policy and calibration. Trainable VAE plus detached DiT gradient performs best; the schedule must match the latent. Block size. Moderate blocks are optimal; larger DiTs tolerate broader blocks. Inference. Strong CFG helps and DiT denoising saturates at about eight steps. 3 · How should the decoder balance reconstruction and generation? Main finding. The decoder should train mostly under noisy and dropped latent
          conditions while retaining clean reconstruction examples. The selected 0.4 / 0.4 / 0.2
          drop–noise–clean mixture reaches 44.7%, and shuffled latents cause the largest degradation,
          confirming that the decoder uses aligned semantic content. Data partition. A 0.4 / 0.4 / 0.2 drop–noise–clean mixture gives the best balance. Guidance and allocation. CFG must match training; a 2B DiT with a 1B decoder is the best 3B split. Decoder size. Accuracy improves monotonically with decoder capacity under a fixed DiT. Latent use. Eight-by-eight steps are optimal; shuffled latents cause the largest degradation. 4 · Do the gains continue as training scales? Main finding. Under controlled training to approximately 210B tokens, Cola-MDM
          achieves the strongest overall scaling. Compressed Cola2-MDM converges more slowly, but
          continues closing its gap to LLaDA after about 150B tokens without saturation. Controlled scaling. Compression retains strong semantic-task scaling while the latent DiT continues to improve. Decoder CFG trend. The optimum remains near −0.9 and drifts gradually toward zero during training. Summary 
The results support the design of Cola2 in three respects. 
Semantic compression: dynamic segmentation and generative supervision keep the
        latents strongly semantic at high compression rates; the former slightly surpasses the
        uncompressed model with an appropriate L max , and the
        latter raises accuracy from 15.7% to 26.7% . 
Reconstruction–generation balance: mixed training dominated by noisy and dropped
        conditions enables the decoder both to faithfully use the latent (shuffling the latent costs 12.2 points ) and to autonomously generate the residual information. 
Continuous–discrete co-adaptation: the information placed in the latent should be
        determined by the decoder; co-training the encoder and decoder yields a 5.9-point gain ,
        and both continue to benefit from more training data, giving the hierarchical model scaling
        trends comparable to or better than AR and LLaDA. Outlook 
Limitations & Future Prospects 
Although our results provide initial evidence for the feasibility, competitiveness, and
        scaling potential of Cola2 , we view the current system as a starting point rather than
        a finished endpoint. 
First, the model remains far from convergence, especially the latent DiT, while the present
        training scale is limited by available resources. Larger models, longer training, and broader
        data and compute budgets are therefore needed to establish its performance ceiling. 
Second, compression reduces the dominant linear and attention FLOPs of the latent prior in
        theory, but end-to-end generation speed also depends on the decoder, CFG branches, latent
        feedback, and hardware utilization. Infrastructure co-design—including specialized kernels,
        cache management, and parallel scheduling—is needed to translate theoretical savings into
        practical speedups. 
Third, Cola2 already shows compatibility with autoregressive and masked-diffusion
        decoders, but their co-training objectives, inference trade-offs, and optimal division of
        labor remain underexplored. 
Finally, the continuous latent space may provide a shared interface between text and other
        continuous modalities such as vision, opening a path toward unified latent representations,
        cross-modal priors, and unified understanding and generation. Cite
