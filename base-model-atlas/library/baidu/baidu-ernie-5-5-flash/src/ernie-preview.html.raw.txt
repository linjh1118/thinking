<section class="showcase-section" data-showcase-section="Game Development">
<h2 id="game-development">Game Development</h2>
<p>ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview supports a broad range of coding tasks, from fixing and iterating on real-world codebases to building complex applications and complete projects. It is particularly capable of long-horizon tasks, with strong planning, execution, and iterative refinement capabilities. We have further improved its game development capabilities through domain-specific training data and a dedicated evaluation framework, focusing on game mechanics implementation, gameplay logic orchestration, runtime state management, and asset pipelines. We have also strengthened its support for major game engines, including Unreal Engine, Unity, and Godot, enabling it to work effectively with each engine’s development paradigms, tools, and workflows. This allows the model to handle practical tasks across the game development process, from game design and gameplay implementation to scene creation.</p>

In a single 11-hour coding task, the model merged assets made for several generations of Unreal Engine into one UE 5.8 project and wrote the entire game in C++. <section class="showcase-section" data-showcase-section="3D Modeling and Design">
<h2 id="3d-modeling-and-design">3D Modeling and Design</h2>
<p>Through specialized training in professional tools such as Blender and CAD, we have significantly enhanced ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview’s capabilities in 3D modeling and design. ERNIE excels at parametric modeling and spatial reasoning. Leveraging tools, it can accurately interpret text descriptions to infer the shapes, proportions, and spatial relationships, and use these insights to create usable 3D models through procedural modeling. It can also flexibly integrate and reuse existing assets, seamlessly incorporating external resources into established workflows to create large-scale, richly detailed 3D scenes.</p>
<div data-showcase-item>
<p><video controls playsinline preload="none" width="100%" poster="/research-assets/ernie-5-5-flash-preview/image-7339b9bb318a.jpg" src="https://ernie-blog.cdn.bcebos.com/research-assets/ernie-5-5-flash-preview/videos/boeing-747-daxing-airport-1080p.a7cf155654d9.mp4"></video></p>
<p class="article-caption"><strong>Building an Airport and an Airliner in Blender</strong>: Two agents running ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview used the Blender Python API, one to build the airport and the other the aircraft. For the airport, the model found layout vector data on a mapping platform by itself, downloaded supporting assets such as vegetation, vehicles and ground support equipment as needed, and built the terminal and airfield with parametric modeling. For the aircraft, it lofted the fuselage and wings from cross-sections and assembled them in modules, then fitted out the cockpit, both passenger decks and the cargo hold with generated assets provided in advance. The model then matched scale, placement and cameras across the two projects, choreographed the showcase animation, rendered it frame by frame on GPU with Cycles and OptiX, and put the video together with FFmpeg. As on a real production, the job was broken into subtasks that progressed separately but stayed coordinated. External assets: vector map data from OpenStreetMap and its contributors (ODbL); vegetation, road and apron PBR textures from Poly Haven (CC0); vehicles and ground support equipment from Sketchfab (CC BY 4.0 or Sketchfab Free Standard); seat, cargo container and vegetation models generated with Tripo AI (accessed through its API, with license). · <a href="https://ernie-blog.cdn.bcebos.com/research-assets/ernie-5-5-flash-preview/videos/boeing-747-daxing-airport-1080p.a7cf155654d9.mp4?responseContentDisposition=attachment" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">View / download video</a></p>
