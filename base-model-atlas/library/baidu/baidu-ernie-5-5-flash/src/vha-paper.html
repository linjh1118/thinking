The design of attention mechanisms in long-context large language models faces an “impossible triangle” among KV cache size, training and prefill FLOPs, and model quality. Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) achieves strong quality but incurs high KV cache overhead; alternative approaches such as Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) and Multi-matrix Factorization Attention (MFA) can compress the cache, but MLA suffers from quality loss while MFA substantially increases training and prefill compute.
We propose Virtual Head Attention (VHA), which simultaneously improves all three vertices through two lightweight linear operations: Q Premix applies a near-identity transformation to queries within each KV group to recover virtual head diversity from halved physical query heads, and Linear Postmix fuses inter-head features via a low-rank I + A ​ B ⊤ I+AB^{\top} residual structure.
Since VHA introduces no nonlinear operations, Premix can be folded into the query projection and Postmix into the output projection at inference time, reducing the model to a standard GQA-2 architecture that directly reuses all existing GQA inference optimizations. With only two KV heads, the group size in large models readily reaches 64, fully exploiting Tensor Core parallelism during decoding.
Across four scales (0.6B, 1.7B, 4B dense, and 8B-A1B MoE) with 4K-context 50B-token pretraining, VHA consistently surpasses the standard GQA baseline at every scale ( + + 0.05% to + + 0.81%), while reducing KV cache by 4 × 4\times vs. GQA-8 at dense scales and 2 × 2\times vs. GQA-4 at the MoE scale, and lowering training and prefill FLOPs by 7.9% (1.7B, S = 4096 S{=}4096 ).
On an industrial 30B-A3B MoE model trained on 500B tokens at 8K context, VHA outperforms both MLA and GQA-4 by + + 0.70 / + + 2.60 % on a 12-benchmark base-model evaluation suite; in a separate matched 10B-A1B MoE comparison after long-context mid-training, VHA attains an average RULER score of 84.4 over 8K–128K versus 73.0 for GQA, while using two rather than four KV heads in the full-attention layers. The source code is available at https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/VHA . 
1 Introduction 
Large language models are rapidly scaling toward longer contexts, with context windows expanding from 2K tokens to 128K and beyond ( Grattafiori et al., 2024 ; Yang et al., 2024 ) . As sequence lengths grow, the attention mechanism ( Vaswani et al., 2017 ) becomes a central efficiency bottleneck in both inference and training. During inference, the key-value (KV) cache grows linearly with sequence length, consuming substantial GPU memory and constraining both serving concurrency and generation latency. During training and prefill, the FLOPs of attention computation scale quadratically with sequence length. Since the design of the attention mechanism jointly determines KV cache size, computational cost, and model expressiveness, it is a critical factor governing the overall efficiency and quality of large language models. 
The design of existing attention mechanisms faces three mutually constraining objectives, forming an “impossible triangle”: KV cache size, training and prefill FLOPs, and model quality. Consider the widely adopted Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) ( Ainslie et al., 2023 ) : GQA with 8 KV heads (GQA-8) achieves strong quality, but incurs a KV cache of 4 KB per layer per token (in BF16). Directly reducing the number of KV heads to 2 (GQA-2) compresses the cache to 1 KB, but dramatically enlarges the group size so that far more query heads must share the same key-value pairs, causing attention diversity to drop sharply and resulting in a 1.00% quality degradation at the 1.7B scale. Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) ( Liu et al., 2024 ) applies low-rank compression to reduce KV cache to 1.12 KB, but yields the lowest quality across multiple model scales. Multi-matrix Factorization Attention (MFA) ( Hu et al., 2025 ) reduces the cache to 1 KB by enlarging the head dimension, achieving quality close to GQA-8, but at the cost of a 49.8% increase in training and prefill FLOPs (1.7B, S = 4096 S{=}4096 ). No existing approach achieves a satisfactory balance across all three dimensions. 
We propose Virtual Head Attention (VHA), which simultaneously improves all three vertices of the impossible triangle through two lightweight linear operations. The core idea of VHA is to halve the number of physical query heads while setting the number of KV heads to only 2. Q Premix then applies a near-identity linear transformation to queries within each KV group, expanding the reduced physical query heads into virtual query heads to recover attention diversity. Linear Postmix applies a low-rank identity-residual transformation ( I + A ​ B ⊤ I+AB^{\top} , r = H q ′ / 2 r{=}H_{q}^{\prime}/2 , e.g., r = 4 r{=}4 at 1.7B) on the attention output, fusing cross-group inter-head features. Since VHA introduces no nonlinear operations, Premix can be directly folded into the query projection matrix and Postmix into the output projection matrix at inference time, reducing the model to a standard GQA-2 architecture that reuses all existing GQA inference optimizations in mainstream serving frameworks such as vLLM ( Kwon et al., 2023 ) and SGLang ( Zheng et al., 2024 ) . Moreover, with only 2 KV heads, the group size in large models readily reaches 64, fully exploiting Tensor Core parallelism during the decoding phase. 
Across four model scales (0.6B, 1.7B, 4B dense, and 8B-A1B MoE) with 4K-context 50B-token pretraining, VHA consistently surpasses the standard GQA baseline at every scale ( + + 0.05% to + + 0.81%), while reducing KV cache by 4 × 4\times vs. GQA-8 at dense scales and 2 × 2\times vs. GQA-4 at the MoE scale, and lowering training and prefill FLOPs by 7.9% (1.7B, S = 4096 S{=}4096 ). On an industrial 30B-A3B MoE model trained on 500B tokens at 8K context, VHA outperforms both MLA and GQA-4 by + + 0.70 / + + 2.60 % on a 12-benchmark base-model evaluation suite, demonstrating scalability to industrial model scale. A separate matched 10B-A1B MoE comparison further extends this advantage to 128K context under more aggressive KV compression. Our main contributions are as follows: 
1. 
We propose VHA, employing Q Premix and Linear Postmix, two lightweight linear operations that simultaneously reduce KV cache and training/prefill FLOPs while improving model quality. 
2. 
VHA uses only linear operations and can be fully absorbed into a standard GQA-2 model at inference time, reusing existing serving frameworks such as vLLM and SGLang with zero additional overhead. 
3. 
Direct inspection of the trained Premix and Postmix matrices reveals two complementary structures (group-conditioned query specialization and structured low-rank cross-head communication) that jointly close the gap of the doubly-compressed configuration; component ablations confirm that neither module is sufficient on its own. 
4. 
Comprehensive evaluations across five scales (0.6B to 30B-A3B MoE) against MHA, GQA, MFA, and MLA establish VHA as the best attention mechanism that is Pareto-optimal on KV cache, training/prefill FLOPs, and downstream quality simultaneously; matched long-context evaluations through 128K further validate this advantage under aggressive KV compression. 
2 Related Work 
2.1 KV Cache Efficient Attention 
Standard Multi-Head Attention (MHA) ( Vaswani et al., 2017 ) maintains independent key and value projections per head, causing the KV cache to grow linearly with the number of heads. Multi-Query Attention (MQA) ( Shazeer, 2019 ) shares a single KV head across all query heads, drastically reducing the cache at the cost of quality. Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) ( Ainslie et al., 2023 ) interpolates between MHA and MQA by allowing multiple query heads to share a group of KV heads, and has been widely adopted in LLaMA 2/3 ( Touvron et al., 2023 ; Grattafiori et al., 2024 ) and Qwen2 ( Yang et al., 2024 ) . 
Several recent designs further compress the KV cache. Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) ( Liu et al., 2024 ) projects KV into a shared low-rank latent space and absorbs the up-projection matrices into Q and O at inference, but requires custom kernels and a non-standard inference pipeline. Multi-matrix Factorization Attention (MFA) ( Hu et al., 2025 ) enlarges the head dimension while reducing the number of KV heads, preserving model capacity under a tight cache budget but substantially increasing training and prefill FLOPs. Tensor Product Attention (TPA) ( Zhang et al., 2025 ) decomposes QKV into context-dependent tensor products. Grouped-head Latent Attention (GTA) ( Sun et al., 2025 ) extends MLA’s global latent space to a group-wise design. Simulated Attention Score (SAS) ( Zheng et al., 2025 ) projects a small number of low-dimensional KV heads into higher-dimensional spaces to simulate more attention heads, an idea related to VHA’s virtual head expansion; however, SAS operates on the KV side whereas VHA virtualizes on the query side. Low-Rank Key-Value Attention (LRKV) ( O’Neill et al., 2026 ) shares a full-rank KV basis across heads and restores head-specific flexibility through low-rank residuals, a form reminiscent of VHA’s Postmix ( I + A ​ B ⊤ I+AB^{\top} ); yet LRKV acts in KV space and its additional structure remains at inference time. Cross-Layer Attention (CLA) ( Brandon et al., 2024 ) and YOCO ( Sun et al., 2024 ) reduce memory by sharing or reusing KV caches across layers. 
Unlike all of the above, VHA employs a richer training-time parameterization that merges exactly back into standard GQA at deployment, requiring no custom kernels or specialized inference procedures. 
2.2 Inter-Head Communication and Virtual Heads 
Talking-Heads Attention ( Shazeer et al., 2020 ) applies learned linear projections across heads before and after softmax, pioneering inter-head communication. Cordonnier et al. (2020) analyze multi-head attention from a collaborative perspective, revealing substantial redundancy among heads. More recently, Explicit Multi-head Attention (MEA) ( Peng et al., 2026 ) introduces a head-level linear composition module for explicit cross-head interaction, and Interleaved Head Attention ( Duvvuri et al., 2026 ) interleaves head representations to enable information exchange. In contrast to these methods, VHA does not mix across heads in the attention weight space; instead, it virtualizes in the query space via Premix and fuses inter-head features in the output space via a low-rank Postmix, with all operations being strictly linear and fully absorbable at inference. 
2.3 Reparameterization 
RepVGG ( Ding et al., 2021 ) uses a multi-branch training architecture ( 3 × 3 + 1 × 1 + identity 3{\times}3+1{\times}1+\text{identity} ) that merges into a single convolution at inference, demonstrating that decoupled training-time parameterization provides implicit regularization at zero inference cost. LoRA ( Hu et al., 2022 ) performs parameter-efficient fine-tuning via low-rank decomposition ( W + B ​ A W+BA ). VHA extends this reparameterization paradigm from a single weight matrix to the head dimension of attention: Premix structurally factorizes the per-virtual-head query projections ( W ~ Q ( i , g ) = W Q ( i ) ​ W g premix \widetilde{W}_{Q}^{(i,g)}=W_{Q}^{(i)}W_{g}^{\mathrm{premix}} ), while Postmix introduces a low-rank identity-residual mixing on the head axis ( I + A ​ B ⊤ I+AB^{\top} ). Both modules are mathematically equivalent in the forward pass to a standard GQA configuration, yet the decoupled parameterization yields distinct optimization dynamics during training and merges back into a standard GQA-2 architecture at inference with zero overhead.

3.1 Preliminaries 
Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) ( Ainslie et al., 2023 ) partitions H q H_{q} query heads into H k ​ v H_{kv} groups, where heads within each group share a common key-value pair. Given input X ∈ ℝ S × h X\in\mathbb{R}^{S\times h} , GQA computes Q = X ​ W Q Q=XW_{Q} , K = X ​ W K K=XW_{K} , V = X ​ W V V=XW_{V} , where W Q ∈ ℝ h × H q ​ d W_{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times H_{q}d} , W K ∈ ℝ h × H k ​ v ​ d W_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times H_{kv}d} , W V ∈ ℝ h × H k ​ v ​ d W_{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times H_{kv}d} . Each query head attends to its group’s key-value pair via scaled dot-product attention ( Vaswani et al., 2017 ) , and all head outputs are concatenated and projected by W O ∈ ℝ H q ​ d × h W_{O}\in\mathbb{R}^{H_{q}d\times h} . During inference, K and V are cached, consuming 4 ​ H k ​ v ​ d 4H_{kv}d bytes per layer per token in BF16. Reducing H k ​ v H_{kv} compresses the cache but enlarges the group size G = H q / H k ​ v G=H_{q}/H_{kv} , forcing more query heads to share the same key-value pair and degrading attention diversity. 
3.2 Overall Design 
VHA halves the number of physical query heads while setting H k ​ v = 2 H_{kv}=2 , then recovers model expressiveness through two lightweight linear operations. Specifically, H q ′ = H q / 2 H_{q}^{\prime}=H_{q}/2 physical query heads and H k ​ v = 2 H_{kv}=2 KV heads yield H v = H k ​ v × H q ′ = H q H_{v}=H_{kv}\times H_{q}^{\prime}=H_{q} virtual heads (matching the original GQA query head count), with W O ∈ ℝ H v ​ d × h W_{O}\in\mathbb{R}^{H_{v}d\times h} identical in size to the original GQA output projection. The resulting KV cache is 4 × 2 × d 4\times 2\times d bytes per layer per token, 4 × 4\times smaller than GQA-8 at dense scales and 2 × 2\times smaller than GQA-4 at MoE scales. 
On top of this configuration, VHA introduces Q Premix , which expands H q ′ H_{q}^{\prime} physical query heads into H v H_{v} virtual query heads to restore attention diversity, and Linear Postmix , which applies a low-rank identity-residual transformation on the attention output to fuse inter-head features. Inference absorption principle. 
VHA uses only linear operations (no nonlinear activations), so that at inference time Premix can be folded into W Q W_{Q} and Postmix into W O W_{O} , reducing the entire model to a standard GQA-2 architecture that directly reuses all existing GQA optimizations in frameworks such as vLLM ( Kwon et al., 2023 ) and SGLang ( Zheng et al., 2024 ) .

Q Premix applies a near-identity linear transformation to physical query heads within each KV group, expanding them into virtual query heads. Why this factorization is useful. 
The structured parameterization brings two benefits over an unconstrained H v H_{v} -head GQA. First, it is parameter- and compute-efficient: the Q path uses H q ′ ​ h ​ d + H k ​ v ​ d 2 H_{q}^{\prime}hd+H_{kv}d^{2} parameters and a comparable number of FLOPs, versus H v ​ h ​ d H_{v}hd for a fully decoupled H v H_{v} -head Q projection. At the 1.7B scale, this yields a roughly 50% reduction in Q-projection parameters and FLOPs (the primary source of VHA’s training-FLOPs reduction). Second, although the forward pass is algebraically equivalent to training a single W ~ Q ( i , g ) \widetilde{W}_{Q}^{(i,g)} , the gradients of W Q ( i ) W_{Q}^{(i)} and W g premix W_{g}^{\mathrm{premix}} are coupled across all groups and physical heads, yielding a different optimization landscape and providing implicit regularization analogous to LoRA ( Hu et al., 2022 ) and RepVGG ( Ding et al., 2021 ) (see Appendix G for a formal analysis). Inference-time absorption. 
Once training is complete and parameters are frozen, for each virtual head ( i , g ) (i,g) we precompute W ~ Q ( i , g ) = W Q ( i ) ⋅ W g premix \widetilde{W}_{Q}^{(i,g)}=W_{Q}^{(i)}\cdot W_{g}^{\mathrm{premix}} , after which VHA reduces to a standard GQA-2 with H v H_{v} independent query projections and H k ​ v = 2 H_{kv}=2 KV heads. 
3.4 Linear Postmix: Low-Rank Identity-Residual Mixing 
While Premix restores virtual head diversity within each KV group, the outputs across different groups still lack cross-group information exchange. Linear Postmix addresses this through a low-rank identity-residual structure.

5 Analysis: How VHA Reparameterization Works 
Beyond task accuracy, VHA should exploit the limited information available under aggressive KV sharing. We therefore inspect the Premix and Postmix matrices of trained VHA models (Premix on the 1.7B, Postmix on the 4B model), asking whether (i) Premix learns a group-conditioned query geometry, (ii) Postmix learns structured low-rank cross-head communication, and (iii) the two jointly close the doubly-compressed gap of Section 4.4 . Premix introduces a learnable per-group query transform W g premix W_{g}^{\mathrm{premix}} before RoPE, initialized near identity. Two trivial outcomes would falsify our hypothesis: (a) W g premix W_{g}^{\mathrm{premix}} remains near identity after training, rendering the module a no-op; or (b) all KV groups converge to the same matrix, degenerating Premix into a group-agnostic shared adapter without group-conditioned specialization. Figure 2 refutes both: panel (a) shows that ‖ W g premix − I ‖ F \|W_{g}^{\mathrm{premix}}-I\|_{F} is substantially nonzero and most pronounced in mid-to-late layers (peak 5.85), and panel (b) shows that the inter-group distance ‖ W g 1 premix − W g 2 premix ‖ F \|W_{g_{1}}^{\mathrm{premix}}-W_{g_{2}}^{\mathrm{premix}}\|_{F} is of comparable magnitude (peak 6.13), yielding a ratio well below the 2 \sqrt{2} value expected under fully independent groups and far above the zero expected under a shared deviation. This quantifies that the two KV groups learn substantially but not independently different query transforms. Premix therefore learns differentiated query probing directions per KV group under a fixed minimal KV cache, realizing genuine group-conditioned virtual-head expansion rather than mere parameter augmentation. Postmix learns structured low-rank cross-head communication. 
Postmix recombines the H v H_{v} attention output heads via the low-rank residual I + B ​ A ⊤ I+BA^{\top} , where entry ( j , i ) (j,i) of | A ​ B ⊤ | |AB^{\top}| encodes the contribution of source head j j to recombined head i i . The central question is whether A ​ B ⊤ AB^{\top} merely performs a per-head rescaling (a strictly diagonal pattern) or genuinely redistributes information across heads (off-diagonal flow). Figure 3 visualizes | A ​ B ⊤ | |AB^{\top}| for the trained 4B model and reveals pronounced off-diagonal flow, ruling out the trivial per-head-rescaling outcome. The dominant pattern is within-group redistribution: Postmix primarily rebalances information among heads sharing the same KV cache, with a sparse but consistent set of cross-group bridges providing inter-group connectivity without additional KV heads. Joint reparameterization closes the doubly-compressed gap. 
Tying these analyses back to the component ablation in Table 4 clarifies the three numbers reported there. Premix-only retains the group-conditioned query geometry of Figure 2 but lacks Postmix’s cross-group repair pathway, hence the − - 0.78% gap at 1.7B. Postmix-only owns the cross-head mixing channels of Figure 3 but operates over collapsed, unspecialized virtual heads, hence the − - 0.14% gap. Only the joint configuration realizes both structures simultaneously, yielding a + + 0.41% advantage over GQA-8 at 25 % 25\% of its KV cache. VHA’s effectiveness therefore arises from the functional complementarity of its two modules: Premix and Postmix are not interchangeable, and neither suffices alone. 
6 Conclusion 
We have presented Virtual Head Attention (VHA), which mitigates the tension among KV cache size, training/prefill FLOPs, and model quality through two lightweight linear operations: Q Premix and Linear Postmix. By halving physical query heads and recovering expressiveness via virtual head expansion and low-rank cross-group mixing, VHA reduces the KV cache to its architectural minimum while simultaneously lowering training FLOPs and improving model quality. Since all added operations are strictly linear, VHA fully absorbs into standard GQA-2 at inference, reusing existing serving optimizations with zero additional overhead. Analysis of trained models reveals that Premix and Postmix are functionally complementary and neither suffices alone, explaining why the joint configuration consistently surpasses the GQA baseline across all five evaluated scales (0.6B to 30B-A3B MoE). A matched 10B-A1B evaluation further shows that VHA maintains this advantage over GQA through 128K context.

Limitations and Future Work 
Our largest validation is at the 30B-A3B MoE scale trained on 500B tokens; scaling VHA to larger MoE models and substantially more training data (e.g., 20T tokens) remains an important direction. We further validate VHA through 128K on a 10B-A1B MoE, while broader long-context architectures and training recipes remain to be explored. VHA’s behavior under reinforcement learning training paradigms is also worth exploring. Finally, VHA is orthogonal to other KV cache compression techniques such as quantization and sparsification, and combining them may yield further inference savings.

1. 
Factored gradient paths. The gradients with respect to A A , B B , and W O W_{O} follow different paths through the computation graph. Identity initialization. With B B initialized to zero, B ​ A ⊤ = 0 BA^{\top}=0 and Y ′ = Y Y^{\prime}=Y at the start of training. Postmix begins as a no-op and gradually learns useful cross-group mixing patterns, avoiding disruption to the early training dynamics.
